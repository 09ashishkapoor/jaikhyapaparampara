When a *Guru* gives *Upadesha* (spiritual instruction), a *Karma* exchange occurs. The *Sadhaka* benefits, but the *Guru* absorbs the *Karma*. If the *Guru* is sincere, this karmic absorption manifests physically — health deteriorates, life shortens. There must be a channel through which this *Karma* can be burned without destroying the *Guru's* body. The answer lies not in money for the *Guru's* personal benefit, but in **feeding the cow and the bull**.

### Why the Cow and Bull Are Purely Spiritual

A TV, a car, a phone — these provide physical benefit. But a cow and a bull provide no financial return. Their presence is entirely spiritual. The more one cleans their dung, carries their feed bags, and serves them, the more one's *Karma* burns — directly, without selfish motive.

The **Kangayam** cow gives only two to three liters of milk — enough for the family, deity offerings, and perhaps a neighbor's glass. There is nothing commercial about her. She is purely spiritual. **Sri Krishna** himself spent most of his life not as a king but wandering with cows in the forests, earning the name **Gopala**. The auric exchange between a *Sadhaka* and a cow is the most grounding experience available on Earth.

### The Story of Karuppan the Bull

While at the peak of Bhairava *Sadhana*, the speaker was driving on an empty highway when a car with flashy lights suddenly swerved, revealing a brilliant bull standing directly in the path. The bull stared at him as he passed. That image consumed his mind.

Research led to the discovery of the **Kangayam** breed and the *Karampasu* — a fully black bull considered an *Amsha* of **Shiva**. People in Tamil Nadu were building temples for legendary black bulls. The desire to own one became all-consuming.

A call came from someone who knew him for his dogs, mentioning a young bull from the famous *Ottakomban* bloodline — 350 kilometers away. The entire connection happened in less than 30 seconds.

Driving alone at dawn, he arrived at a small family's home. They were astonished that he came alone, knowing nothing about bulls, with no guide. His response: *"You think I'm alone? There are a lot of entities in this car continuously guiding me."*

While standing there, an eight-month-old calf — tied next to the bull — walked up, turned around, and passed her urine onto his feet. The owner recognized this as an ancient *Nimitta* (omen) of divine permission — the same sign used in ancient times to determine where to dig wells that would last centuries. The calf was insisting on being taken.

After a full day spent in the town — visiting homes of Kangayam breeders, experiencing an atmosphere of extreme calm and peace that felt like stepping back 10,000 years — he agreed to take both. The bull was named **Karuppan**. The calf was named **Kamakshi**. They arrived home on a *Pradosham* day during *Brahma Muhurta*. Karuppan was the first physical occupant of the *Peetha*.

### Gau Seva: The Greatest Karma Burner

Post mantra *Japa*, post *Homa*, as long as one is physically present on this planet, **Gau Seva** (service to the cow) is the most vital practice. Even if one cannot afford anything — feed the cow a banana. Go to an *ashrama* where a *Sadhaka* keeps a cow, clean the *Gaushala*, remove the dung. Do not pay a business establishment. Serve the cow of a *Guru* directly.

This is not business. This is direct *Karma* burning. Every *Upadesha* given, every mantra shared — the karmic exchange completes when the *Sadhaka* feeds the cow. Without this channel, the *Guru* burns the karma directly and suffers physically.

### On Choosing a Guru and Remaining Loyal

A case study illustrates the danger of *Guru*-shopping. A family with **Narasimha** as their *Kula Devata* received *Upadesha* to restrict Bhairava *Sadhana* to *Ashtamis* only — because Narasimha, who exists solely to protect his devotees regardless of conditions, had not given permission for the new path.

After receiving this guidance, the family posted anonymously on another channel asking if Narasimha *Kula* members could do Bhairava *Sadhana* — without mentioning the specific *Upadesha* they had already received. This was disrespectful to both *Gurus*.

The principles of *Guru* loyalty:

*   **Do your homework before choosing**: Listen to all discourses. Does the *Guru* extract esoteric meanings others cannot? Does he make sense? Then commit.
*   **Once chosen, be like stone**: The deity sits in stone because stone is unmoving for 10,000 years. Your commitment to the *Guru* must be equally unmoving.
*   **Be fair to all Gurus**: If you seek a second opinion, tell the full history — who you went to, what *Upadesha* was given. Do not say "someone said." That *someone* might be embodying the deity itself.
*   **Do not Guru-hop**: If you have taken *Upadesha*, embody it fully. Constant movement means the deity has no stable seat within you. You cannot seat a deity in an apple that rots — you need to be the stone.
*   **The deity as Guru is final**: If the deity is your *Guru*, do not quest after humans for *Upadesha*. Find a human *Guru* and be loyal, or commit fully to the deity. Both are valid — but switching between them disrespectfully is not.

### The Guru Sthana Must Be Stable

The *Guru Sthana* (the seat of the *Guru*) is like the *Asana* — it must be stable as a rock for the deity to embody the disciple. In Kali Yuga, where a thousand *Gurus* serve lakhs of *shishyas*, this stability has collapsed. But for the individual *Sadhaka*, the commitment must remain absolute.

Before choosing, think deeply. After choosing, stop questioning. Embody the *Upadesha* completely. And when karma burns — feed the cow.

**Bhairava Kalike Namostute.**
