<h3 style="color:var(--accent-bright);margin-top:0;">Spiritual Insight</h3>
<p>Mahashivratri, the "Night of Shiva," is one of the most significant observances in the Bengali spiritual calendar. Gurudev explains that while traditionally a vow for women (brata) to resolve financial crises and please both Mahadev and Mother Durga, men have increasingly joined the practice. The night is divided into four distinct phases of worship: Ishan (10 PM), Aghore (midnight), Bama (2 AM), and Sadyojata (4 AM). The famous story of the hunter (Vyadh) who accidentally worshipped Shiva while hiding in a Bael tree demonstrates that sincere devotion—even unknowingly offered—can transform one's destiny. The Sadyojata form represents Shiva as a newborn, reminding us that even the fierce Chamunda's rage could be quelled by maternal instinct. Gurudev's practical advice for marriage-seekers—tying yellow thread around a Peepal tree and offering water daily—reflects the accessible spirituality he teaches. His core message remains: women should worship Shiva, men should worship Mother Durga, for their union creates the divine "light" that fulfills all needs.</p>
