Asked why ordinary people should keep the month, he answers that it belongs to Shiva.</p>

<h3 style="color:var(--accent-bright);margin-top:0;">Spiritual Insight</h3>
<p>Gurudev answers the question about <a href="/articles/topics/rituals-worship-festivals/">Shravan</a> from inside his own practice rather than from a festival calendar. He begins with the Tantric line <em>"Maghayam Shravanayam va Revatyam va visheshatah"</em> - the months of Magh and Shravan joined with the Revati Nakshatra, a combination in which, he says, "we can perform any work" - and only then tells the <em>Samudra Manthan</em> story that gives the month its purity. The narrative arc he draws is worth noting: the churning yields riches, then <em>Halahala</em>, then <em>Amrita</em> guarded by Mohini, and the places where the pot rested become the Kumbh Mela. His practical instruction is smaller and warmer than the cosmology - in this month of Devadhidev Mahadev, carry water, fast, and pour it on Baba's head for the good of your family. The talk opens with the Guru Purnima puja and homa at Shantikunj Rajpur Mahashashan; the date named in the video refers to that year's observance.
