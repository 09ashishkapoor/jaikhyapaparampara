<h2>The Science of Fasting: Kam vs. Nishkam</h2>

There are two types of worship: **Kam Puja** (desire-based) and **Nishkam Puja** (selfless). 
- If you have a specific desire—like a son's recovery or a good husband—fasting is traditionally part of the vow. This is why many girls fast on *Shivratri*; they seek a husband like Shiva.
- However, for **Nishkam Puja**, where you simply love and follow God without demands, fasting is not necessary. It is better to eat a light meal and focus on meditation than to suffer from acidity and gas while trying to pray. Even the Goddess here is offered tea and biscuits in the morning; if we drink tea, shouldn't she?

<h2>The Power of Habit: Abhyasa Yoga</h2>

<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Humans are slaves to habit, which the *Gita* calls *Abhyasa Yoga*. If you make it a habit to eat only what is necessary, your body remains light for spiritual practice. I eat rice with only one curry—no spices, no richness. Why? Because eating too much causes sleepiness and digestive issues that ruin one's meditation posture. A seeker must eat less to call upon God effectively.</span>
</div>

<h2>What is Satvik Food?</h2>

Satvik food is not complicated. It is often as simple as **Akshiddha bhat**—rice steamed with vegetables and a little pure ghee. It is the food of the sages. In the modern market, finding pure food is hard, but choosing fruits, milk, and simple grains helps maintain the body's trillions of bacteria, which are scientifically responsible for our vitality.

<h2>The Four Gs of Spiritual Satisfaction</h2>

Lord Krishna said he is most satisfied where the four **'G's** are present:
1. **Ganga** (Holy water/purity)
2. **Gayatri** (Mantra/wisdom)
3. **Gita** (Scripture/guidance)
4. **Govinda** (The Divine Name)
