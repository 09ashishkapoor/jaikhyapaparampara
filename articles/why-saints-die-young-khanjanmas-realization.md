Some souls are born specifically for that one purpose—to achieve God-realization. Once that is done, there is no more reason to stay. Let me explain with examples: our Jagadguru Shankaracharya lived for 29 years. He traveled all over India, debated the greatest scholars, and established four cardinal monasteries. In just 29 years! How many people accomplish that in eighty or ninety years? After having a vision of the Mother in a specific form, after which no one lives anymore. That is the nature of the Mother's grace. When she grants you her vision, she takes you back. It is not a punishment; it is a completion. Examples are our Jagadguru Shankaracharya, Swami Vivekananda, and Annada Thakur—all fell into this category of Khanjanmas Jivans.</span>

<h3 style="color:var(--accent-bright);margin-top:0;">Spiritual Insight</h3>
<p>The concept of Khanjanmas—births designated solely for God-realization—explains why realized saints die young. Once they have glimpsed the Mother Divine, their earthly contract is complete. Their bodies may continue functioning for a brief span, but their consciousness has transcended this realm. This is not death but completion, not loss but return to the divine source. Ramakrishna, Vivekananda, Shankaracharya, and Annada Thakur exemplify this truth: age becomes irrelevant once the Mother calls.</p>
