## Introduction: The Sanctity of the Mother’s Room

Many devotees visit Tarapith with profound devotion, yet many return with more than just blessings—they unknowingly accumulate sins. In a recent discourse, Gurudev Shyama Khyapa addressed a common grievance: the aggressive behavior of priests (*Pundas*) who force devotees' heads onto the Mother’s feet for money.

Gurudev’s response was clear and firm: "Not at all. Why should the dust of your feet enter the Mother’s room? It is not right. This is the Mother’s room; She resides there. Where She stays, it is not right for the dust from your feet to be present because She eats and resides there."

## The Mystery of the Brahmasila

Ma Tara sits upon a sacred stone known as the **Brahmasila**. Legend says this is the stone where Ma Durga nursed Lord Mahadev. While various opinions exist about its origin—Assam, Tibet, or China—its spiritual power is undisputed but delicate.

Bama Baba (Bamakhepa) gave a stern warning regarding this stone:
> "Never look at that stone. If you look at it, you might go mad. And if you touch it, you might go blind."

Gurudev laments that today, the power of that stone has diminished because so many people touch it, driven by the greed of priests and their own lack of humility. 

## How to Visit Ma Tara: The Path of the Beggar Child

The correct way to approach the Divine Mother is not with pride or social standing, but with the humility of a child.

- **Go as a Beggar:** Approach Ma Tara saying, "I have come as a beggar child, Mother, have mercy on me. Give me some alms, Mother."
- **Avoid the Inner Sanctum:** There is no spiritual necessity to enter the inner room or touch the deity. Seeing the Mother from the outside is sufficient.
- **Maintain Humility:** Today’s trend of taking pictures and "falling all over the Mother" is a sign of ego, not devotion. Such actions result in bringing home a "pile of sins" rather than grace.

## The Rule of Tarapith: "First Bama, Then Tara"

A critical rule for any visitor to Tarapith is the order of worship. 

**"First Bama, then Tara."**

Before seeking the *darshan* of Ma Tara, one must first visit Bamakhepa’s temple. Offer your respects there, take the dust of his feet, and offer a garland. As Gurudev beautifully puts it: 
*"If you pull the ear, the head follows. If you worship Bama, the Mother will come. First Bama eats, then Tara eats."*

## The Different Forms of Tara

While Ugratara resides within the main temple, there are seven other Taras within the Tarapith area, including:
- **Ekjata:** Residing in the cremation ground.
- **Nil Saraswati**
- **Nitya**
- **Vajra**

Most visitors are unaware of these deeper spiritual structures and focus only on the main temple, often misled by those who prioritize financial gain over spiritual correctness.

## Dangers of the Cremation Ground

Gurudev warns against the new practices of bringing items like coins or incense from the funeral pyres back home. Doing so, he warns, can lead to ruin in business and personal life. "You went to the great cremation ground of Tarapith and performed rituals in the names of living people. Now whatever happens, happens."

## Conclusion: Talk to Her from a Distance

Tarapith itself is the field of Tara. She is everywhere. There is no need to push or force your way into the inner sanctum. 

"She is everywhere there. See her from a distance. Talk to Her from a distance; She will hear you."

By following the path shown by realized souls like Bama Baba and Gurudev Shyama Khyapa, devotees can ensure that their pilgrimage to Tarapith brings true spiritual alms rather than the weight of unintended sins.
