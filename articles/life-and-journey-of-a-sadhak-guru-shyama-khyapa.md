<h2>Early Life and Struggles</h2>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">There isn't much to say. According to the rules of those days, I had my sacred thread ceremony (*paita*) at the age of nine or ten. From then on, I became a priest, and calls for worship rituals started coming in. Most importantly, my family's financial situation was so bad that we could only eat if I performed a ritual. My three brothers and I would perform rituals to somehow manage the household. We stayed together and ate together. As long as my mother was alive, there was no trouble. After she passed away, I took up *Tantra Sadhana*, and all my obstacles disappeared. While my mother was alive, I couldn't do it. She didn't allow me to enter this line. After she died, I became free of all bonds and entered this path.</span>
</div>

Before that, I did some business—a large export business—and was reputable in the legal profession. I had about 50 to 60 boys working for me. My joy was in providing for them. Then, in the wheel of time, God gave me a dream: "Leave all this. Your job is to establish and worship me, Mother Kali." I gave up everything—cars, property, everything—and started worshipping the Mother. I was around 45 years old when I established the Mother and started worshipping her. I then plunged into intense *sadhana*. I don't think anyone has done such intense *sadhana* in modern times. The result is that today the whole world respects me.

<h2>The Concept of Yugavatar</h2>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Interviewer:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Gurudev, I’ve heard you say that a man becomes an incarnation (*avatar*) or becomes God...</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">No, a man is a *Yug Avatar* (Incarnation of the Age). Ramakrishna is called a *Yug Avatar*, as is Bamdev. They aren't just called "Avatar"—it’s *Yugavatar*. An incarnation for the age.</span>
</div>

<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Interviewer:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">How do you feel when thousands of devotees come and hold your feet, calling you Father or God?</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">There is no "feeling" as such. I don't have that ego that "I am someone important." I never nurture that. Their belief is the biggest thing: "You can do it; everything is possible through you." I don't want to break that belief.</span>

<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Interviewer:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Tell us about an experience that caused you great pain.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Because I lived in cremation grounds (*shmashan*), none of my relatives liked me. They thought because I lived there, I was doing bad things. This was a great sorrow for me, and I stopped going to my relatives' houses. I prefer the cremation ground. One who sees the cremation ground and the home as equal is a true *sadhu*. A person who finds the same joy in the *shmashan* as in the home—meaning both are equal to him.</span>

<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Interviewer:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">How do you see Science and *Tantra*? Are they related?</span>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">

But if you want to see something supernatural, *Tantra* shows what science cannot.</span>
