<h3 style="color:var(--accent-bright);margin-top:0;">Spiritual Insight</h3>
<p>Jagadhatri, the "Holder of the World," reveals herself to those who walk the sacred geography with eyes of devotion. Guru Shyama Khyapa's vision of her at Dehu Kamandala during his Narmada Parikrama demonstrates how pilgrimage becomes revelation—the outer journey mirrors and catalyzes the inner awakening. The meticulous details of the Arghya offering (108 Durva grass stalks, 108 rice grains, Alata and Bel leaves with red sandalwood) encode deep tantric symbolism: the number 108 represents the 108 vital energy channels (nadis) in the human body, while the combination of elements balances the cosmic forces. The Guru's recitation of the Chandi—even while struggling with breathlessness—shows the tantric understanding that the Mother's power flows through the voice regardless of physical limitation. His explanation of Sandhi Puja as a "non-time" when only Chamunda receives worship reveals the esoteric dimension of Durga Puja, where the fierce form of the Goddess claims the liminal moments between day and night, between forms, between worlds.</p>
