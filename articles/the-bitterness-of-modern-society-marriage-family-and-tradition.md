**Interviewer:** Greetings, Gurudev. In the old days, a boy would tell his mother he's bringing her a "maidservant" when he gets married. How logical is that today?

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Those days are gone. The term "Dasi" (devotee/servant) used to be used for daughters-in-law to signify their entry into a new life of service to the family. Now, marriages happen through phone chats and quick trips to Kalighat. Parents are left out of the most important decisions, causing them great pain.</span>
</div>

In the past, horoscopes and "Gotra" (lineage) were carefully matched for scientific reasons—to prevent "blood defects" and ensure healthy children. Now, they sit in a park, eat peanuts, and decide to marry. The result? Out of 100 people who come to me, 70 or 80 are divorce cases. My ears are tired of hearing them.

I see people paying 50,000 Rupees for a "Rudraksha" thinking it will save a broken marriage. Love and commitment aren't found through "Tantra" or business; they are built on the bond between husband and wife. Now, careers come first, and children are neglected or planned too late.

**Interviewer:** The foundation of relationships seems weak today.

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">It is a "trial and error" culture. Live-in relationships have no liability, and marriage is no longer seen as a sacred commitment between two families. We are losing the essence of what a family is. I have a bitterness about this state of society. My mantra for my disciples is always **World Peace and Human Welfare**, but it is hard to bridge the gap when people have lost their roots.</span>
</div>
