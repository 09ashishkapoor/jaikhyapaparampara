<h2>The Arrival from China: Vashistha Muni</h2>

The great sage Vashistha Muni was directed by the Mother in a dream to bring her from China (*Chin Desh*). There, she was worshipped with rituals that initially repulsed him—*Chin Achara*. However, he learned that Mother accepts all sincere devotion, even from the sea pirates of the East who worshipped her to safeguard their voyages. Vashistha eventually brought her form to Tarapith and installed the sacred stone.

<h2>The Brahma Shila and the Nectar of Nursing</h2>

The core of Tarapith is the *Brahma Shila*. This stone represents the moment Mother Tara nursed Lord Shiva to save him from the agonizing poison of the *Samudra Manthan*. It is said that looking at this stone directly can cause blindness or insanity; it is a force beyond human comprehension. Even in my Guru Bama Khepa's time, this was a strictly guarded secret.

<h2>Bama Khepa: The Mad Bhairava</h2>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Bama Khepa was born on *Shivaratri* and was the living manifestation of Maheshwara. He roamed the Tarapith cremation grounds as a "mad" saint, completely one with Mother Tara. His bond was that of a son and a mother—unfiltered and divine. He performed *sadhana* over corpses and attained the highest powers, yet remained simple enough to ask station masters for the name of the stop.</span>
</div>

<h2>Divine Vibrations and Disciples</h2>

Great souls like Swami Nigamananda and Durga Prasanna Paramhansa were drawn to Bama Baba's light. Nigamananda, seeking to communicate with his deceased wife, was instead steered toward the path of a Paramhansa by Baba's stern but loving guidance. Whether Bama Baba consumed alcohol or milk was irrelevant to his true nature; he was a living Shiva who could stop trains with a thought to receive his devotees.
