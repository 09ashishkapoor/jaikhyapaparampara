<h2>The Encounter with Mother Annapurna</h2>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Talking about Sadhak Ramprasad is a bit audacious—he was such a great practitioner of Tantra. He could be called the founder of Kali Puja in Bengal, along with Krishnananda Agambagish.

Once, it so happened that he used to perform puja at home on every Amavasya (new moon). One morning, as he was about to go for his bath, a lady arrived and asked, "Is this Ramprasad's home?" He replied, "Yes, what do you need?" She said, "I want to hear a song from Ramprasad." Ramprasad replied, "I am Ramprasad, but I cannot sing for you right now. It's Amavasya, the Mother's puja. I cannot sing until the puja is over. Why don't you wait on the porch while I finish my bath? After the puja, I will sing for you."

He went to bathe. The door to the Mother's room—Goddess Kali's room—was open. When he returned, he saw no one. He asked his wife, "A lady was sitting here; where did she go?" She replied, "I can't say."

Ramprasad was taken aback. Then, he saw something written on the door: <em>"Ramprasad, I am Mother Annapurna. I came all the way from Kashi to hear your song. But unfortunately, I didn't get to hear it. Well, if you ever can, sing a song for me."</em>

Seeing this, Ramprasad lost his mind with devotion. He began banging his head against the door where it was written. He told his wife, "Quickly arrange for the puja. After the puja, I am leaving for Benaras (Kashi)." In those days, people traveled by boat along the Ganges.

Just as he was leaving—as he stepped onto the boat—a divine voice (Akashbani) spoke from above: <em>"Ramprasad, you don't need to come to Kashi. Sing from where you are, and I will listen. I am everywhere. You don't need to come to Kashi."</em> So, he gave up his plan and sang some songs for the Mother right there. These things happened in his life. I'm just sharing fragments of his story.</span>
</div>

<h2>The Profound Meaning of Ramprasad’s Songs</h2>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">He wrote many songs. A few are quite powerful, like:
<em>"Dub de re mon Kali bole, hridi-rotna-kor ogadh jole..."</em> (Dive deep, O mind, in the name of Kali, into the bottomless waters of the heart's ocean...)

He says that the "ocean of jewels" is never empty; if you don't find anything in two or four dives, dive deep into the Kulakundalini. Here, he composed the song based on the Kulakundalini. If you don't find Her in one dive, keep diving. Go where the source is. Then he says: <em>"Gyan-somudrer majhe ro-mon, shanti-rupa mukta-phole..."</em> (In the midst of the ocean of knowledge lies the pearl of peace...)

He who has knowledge has peace. One who is ignorant has no peace. That's why he says that in the ocean of knowledge, you will find the pearl of peace. You will find it through devotion, just as Shiva made a pact with the Mother—that he would lie at Her feet and never rise. This is called the "Shiva-Pact." Mahakal made this pact with the Mother. Ramprasad expressed this in his song.

Then he says: <em>"Deher modhye choi ripu ma, bol ama ki shamal kori..."</em> (There are six enemies within the body, Mother, tell me how to manage them...) He's talking about the six ripus (vices): lust, anger, greed, attachment, pride, and envy. He realized them deeply and said: <em>"I feel like killing them all with poison."</em> He couldn't control them, so he said, "Let's just kill them with poison." But if he poisoned them, he would die too!

In another place, he writes: <em>"Deher modhye choi kumbhir ma, ahar-lobhe shodye chole..."</em> (There are six crocodiles in the body, Mother, always moving in search of food...) He says, <em>"Apply the turmeric of conscience (Bibek) to your body, and they won't touch you once they smell it."</em> He's saying to awaken your conscience. Lord Buddha also said this—awaken your conscience. Ramprasad realized this too: "Apply the turmeric of conscience to your body, and the six crocodiles won't touch you."</span>
</div>

<h2>The Universal Mother: From Tara to Khoda</h2>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">He had to write about Tara as well, not just Kali. He wrote many songs about Tara.
<em>"Tumi jeno bhojer baji, jenechi jenechi tara..."</em> (You are like a magician's trick, I have known you, Mother Tara...)

<em>"Moge bole bhora tara, got bole phiringi jara..."</em> (The Mugs call You 'Bhora Tara,' the Europeans call You 'God'...)
The Magh pirates, when they went to sea, used to worship Tara. He realized this. He says: <em>"Khoda bole dake tomay, Mogol-Pathan-Syeed-Gazi... Jenechi jenechi tara, tumi jeno bhojer baji."</em> (The Mughals, Pathans, Syeeds, and Gazis call You 'Khoda'... I have known You, Tara, You are like a magician's trick.)

Whoever sees the Mother, sees Her in their own way. <em>"Shilpi bole Bishwakarma, bador bole nayer majhi."</em> (The artist calls You 'Vishwakarma,' the boatman calls You 'Bador.') "Bador" is what boatmen call the spirit of the sea. He composed these life-giving songs that can truly alleviate human suffering. No matter how much pain someone is in, if they listen to these songs, I believe they will forget their grief.</span>
</div>

<h2>The Attainment of Brahmagyan and Final Journey</h2>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">When Krishnananda Agambagish initiated him, he said, "You must practice Tara Sadhana." Ramprasad said, "I worship Kali, why should I worship Tara, Gurudev?" The Guru replied, "You must worship Tara. You must perform all spiritual practices."

The Guru gave him the mantra and he performed Shab-sadhana (meditation on a corpse) in the crematorium. He performed it for Mother Tara. When his sacrifice (Yajna) was complete, Mother Tara appeared. She asked, "Ramprasad, what do you want?" Ramprasad said, "Mother, I want nothing but Brahmagyan (supreme knowledge)."

Usually, when someone attains Brahmagyan, they don't live long. Mother Tara blessed him and said, "So be it. I grant you Brahmagyan." After that, he didn't live for more than a month. At the end, he built a Kali idol with his own hands, painted it, and performed the puja at night.

The next morning, when it was time to immerse the Mother's idol in the Ganges, he went into the river with the idol. He dipped into the water and never came back. His body was never found. There are a few people whose bodies were never found—Ramprasad was one, and Tailanga Swami was another. I've shared as much as a person like me can about this great saint.</span>
</div>
