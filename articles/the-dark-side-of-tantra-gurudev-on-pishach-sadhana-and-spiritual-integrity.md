<h2>The Realm of the Spirits</h2>

Pishach sadhana cannot be done everywhere. It requires specific locations: crematoriums, open fields, or 'shunagars'—houses where no normal domestic activities occur. It involves 21 days of isolation within a crematorium. I have witnessed practitioners consuming brain matter from half-burnt corpses mixed with alcohol during these rituals. This is the path of darkness.

**Karna Pishach** (whispering in the ear) is particularly treacherous. Once this spirit is mastered, it whispers answers to your questions, but it will never let you leave. It is an obsession that often ends in the practitioner's death at the hands of the very spirit they tried to control.

<h2>Why I Chose the Mother Over the Spirits</h2>

I know the entire process for these dark rituals, but I have never performed them. Why? Because they make one low in the eyes of God. A Sattvic seeker seeks liberation and the Divine Mother, not a spirit for quick, cheap power. These tamasic practices give results fast, but they are like a debt that must be paid in blood.

Many have tried to influence me to take up these practices because of the power I hold, but I refuse. I will never misuse my power. I am with God, and I will stay with God. True mastery over the self and devotion to Ma Tara is many times more profound than controlling a ghost.

<h2>Life in the Rajpur Crematorium</h2>

I have been practicing here for 32 years. When I first came, the village was secluded, and the pyres were wooden. Every crematorium has a **Shoshan-rokkhok** (Protector) and a **Shoshan-dwari** (Gatekeeper). Meeting the Protector can be terrifying; our rule is that if you encounter him, you must offer a few drops of blood from your finger to the soil to ensure your survival.

Ma Tara protects me. I have a relationship with her that I cannot fully describe. If I am in deep trouble, I simply hold onto her door, and she arranges everything. Once, when my electricity was cut, she sent a stranger from miles away at 4 AM to provide the exact amount needed for the bill.

<h2>The Lesson of the Dogs and Spirits</h2>

In this world, what you see is not everything. Dogs are often manifestations of Bhairavs; Bama Khyapa himself had a dog he called 'Kalu Babu' and treated as a human. We also know that when a dog cries in a certain way, a death is certain. 

Spirits exist—good souls smell like flowers, while bad souls have a foul odor. Whether they are unfulfilled or just of a lower nature, they are part of the reality of the cremation ground. But for a wise person, it is better not to go into these spirit-based practices. It is a waste of a human life that could be used to attain the Divine.
