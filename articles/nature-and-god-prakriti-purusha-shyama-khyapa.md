<p class="transcript-context">Guru Shyama Khyapa opens this short episode by rejecting the idea that God created us and asking the disciple to seek the theory of creation instead. He sets Prakriti, the world we live in, against Purusha, the deities Narayan, Mahadev, and Brahmadev, argues that both are great and that Nature takes revenge when she is exploited, and closes with the sixteen-word Harinam.</p>

<h3 style="color:var(--accent-bright);margin-top:0;">Spiritual Insight</h3>
<p>This is one of Gurudev's clearest statements of <a href="/articles/topics/spiritual-teachings-philosophy/">Prakriti and Purusha</a>, and it begins by refusing a comfortable answer: asked whether God created us, he says "that is nonsense," and sends the seeker to the theory of creation instead. His working definition is concrete - Prakriti is the world we live in, with storms, earthquakes, and decay as her work, while Purusha is the three deities who control her but do not always succeed. The household image he uses, husband and wife as Purusha and Prakriti, is offered "comparatively," and he is explicit that neither is small. The practical edge is his warning that exploitation of Nature brings her revenge, and his observation that people call on God in a catastrophe but never call on Nature to be calm. He ends where he usually ends, with the sixteen-word <a href="/articles/topics/sadhana-tantra-mantra/">Harinam</a> as the one practice for Kali Yuga. For the same theme in a longer key see <a href="/articles/is-formal-worship-mandatory-guru-shyama-khyapa-on-rituals-nature-and-brahman/">whether formal worship is mandatory</a> and <a href="/articles/mutilation-of-nature-spiritual-warning/">the mutilation of Nature</a>.</p>
