<p class="transcript-context">Asked on Kali Puja to explain the difference between Kali and Mahakali, Guru Shyama Khyapa says there is none and that the difference is only in our restless minds, tracing one goddess through Durga, Kali, Tara, and Mahakali and reading the name itself out of Kala, time and death. He then warns that the Dasa Mahavidyas are not for household worship, quoting the Tantric rule of the cremation ground, the open field, and the empty house, and he says the observances of the new moon are passing out of common knowledge.</p>

<h3 style="color:var(--accent-bright);margin-top:0;">Spiritual Insight</h3>
<p>Two teachings sit inside one Kali Puja conversation. The first is non-dual and quietly corrective: asked about <a href="/articles/dasha-mahavidya-maa-kali-first-goddess-tantra-shyama-khyapa/">Kali and Mahakali</a>, Gurudev says the difference is "in our minds" and that the many names are inks of different colours writing the same thing. His etymology is the memorable part - from <em>Kala</em>, time and death, comes Kali, so Kali Puja is the worship of death, and Gauri became Mahakali in the lap of Mahakala. The second is a firm ritual prohibition: the <a href="/articles/one-should-not-see-stars-in-amat-why-gurudev-warns-against-home-dash-mahavidya-puja/">Dasa Mahavidyas are not for household worship</a>, because the Tantric rule places them in the cremation ground, the open field, and the empty house, and because what is invoked comes home with the deity. He is equally clear that the knowledge is being lost - people no longer know whether it is <em>Amavasya</em> or <em>Purnima</em> - and ends by saying it is wiser for ordinary people not to associate with "people like us." Take the article as descriptive teaching about a lineage practice, not as instruction to perform Tantric worship yourself.</p>
