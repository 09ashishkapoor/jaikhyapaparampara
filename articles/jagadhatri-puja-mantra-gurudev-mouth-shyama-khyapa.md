<p>This intimate conversation captures Gurudev in a moment of physical vulnerability—recovering from a procedure with a catheter in place—yet still generously sharing the sacred <strong>Jagadhatri Puja mantra</strong> from the Chandi Path. His recitation of <em>"Singhasthā shashi-shekharā..."</em> reveals the Tantric form of the Goddess: seated on a lion, with the crescent moon adorning her head, four arms bearing conch, discus, bow and arrows, and three eyes radiating divine power. The title <strong>"Durga Durgati-Harini"</strong>—remover of all distress—takes on special significance as Gurudev himself seeks healing. His explanation of the <strong>Siddha Kavach</strong> preparation—written at midnight and soaked in specially prepared water while meditating on the deity—offers rare insight into authentic Tantric practice. The unity of all forms of Durga, whether Nine Durgas or Fifty-one, through a single Ghat, reflects the essential non-dual nature of the Divine Mother.</p>
