<h2>The Promise of Loknath Baba</h2>

High-level practitioners who reach a certain stage through intense *sadhana* develop a formidable power within. Only then can they truly say, "I am he." 

Take **Loknath Baba**, for example. He said: *"In battle, in the forest, in the jungle—wherever you find yourself in danger, remember me, and I will protect you."* Very few have the courage or the spiritual realization to make such a declaration. He didn't need to be physically present; he could sense the call of a sincere devotee in distress and immediately arrange for their protection.

<h2>Righteousness and Divine Protection</h2>

However, this promise is for the righteous. You cannot commit a crime and expect divine protection. God protects those who fall into trouble unknowingly or live truthfully. When you call out with a sincere heart, "Father, I am in danger, please protect me," the Divine hears that call.

The power of the Divine can enter a *sadhak*. If Mother Tara's power enters you through your practice, your words carry weight. But this power must never be used for harm. It is given solely for the welfare of humanity. As long as you act for the good of others, the Divine is with you.

<h2>The Third Eye and the Evolution of Man</h2>

Bhabapagla used to say that humans have the potential to become gods or avatars. Divine beings don't just descend; humans evolve into them. We have a "third eye," or spiritual insight, that remains closed for most. Only the Divine or a true Guru can help open the *Agya Chakra*. When that opens, your perception changes, and you begin to see beyond the physical world.

<h2>The True Sadhak</h2>

A true *sadhak* is quiet and unassuming. They don't seek fame or grand rituals. Service is the highest form of religion. A *sadhak*’s true work is the welfare of others. Even the "God Particle" that scientists speak of reflects this—immense potential that manifests when it interacts with the right forces. 

The Divine resides within each of us. We may be distracted, but it is there. Our primary task is to have faith. From that faith, everything else—devotion, respect, and love—will grow.
