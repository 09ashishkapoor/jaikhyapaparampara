<h3 style="color:var(--accent-bright);margin-top:0;">Spiritual Insight</h3>
<p>The Omkar—or Pranava—represents the primordial sound from which all creation emerges, the sonic vibration that existed before the manifested universe and will remain after its dissolution. Guru Shyama Khyapa's teaching that "I am He" cuts through the illusion of separation that has plagued seekers for millennia. The three questions—"Who am I? Where have I come from? What is my purpose?"—form the tripod upon which all genuine self-inquiry rests. The Guru's revelation that the eyes represent truth while the ears represent falsehood suggests a hierarchy of perception: direct seeing transcends the hearsay and rumor that dominate the mental landscape. His description of the "black hole in our minds" that absorbs sorrow and pain offers a yogic neuroscience: the brain's natural mechanism for processing trauma parallels the cosmic black holes that swallow stars. The prescription is simple yet profound—rise before dawn when the world is quiet, chant Omkar from Muladhara to Hriday-Akasha, and let the sun's rays purify both body and soul. In an age of digital distraction where, as the Guru notes, mobile phones offer 10% benefit with 90% harm, returning to this ancient practice becomes not merely spiritual preference but urgent necessity.</p>
