<p class="transcript-context">A delegation from the Vishwa Sanatan Sanskriti Parishad, led by its president Abhijit Bose, comes to Shantikunj during Devi Paksha to honour Guru Shyama Khyapa and seek his blessings. Gurudev says the visit fulfils a long-held wish he could not act on himself because of his ailments, blesses the organisation's work for Sanatanis across India, and Bose speaks of carrying that blessing to the coming Kumbh Mela in Haridwar.</p>

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Today, as the President of the Vishwa Sanatan Sanskriti Parishad, I, Abhijit Bose, have come to Sadhak Shyama Khyapa Baba. As Pitru Paksha has ended and Devi Paksha has begun—Devi Paksha is currently ongoing—at this auspicious juncture, we have come to seek his blessings and take refuge in him. I will say only one thing: if we have the blessings of a realized soul like him, we can take this organization much further and give a new direction to India. The work we are doing for Sanatanis in Kolkata, with his blessings, we will be able to unite Sanatanis across India. And I want his hand to be on my head, so that with his blessings, we can work for India. This year, the Kumbh Mela is going to be held in Haridwar. We are going to organize a major program in Haridwar with his blessings, where countless Naga Sadhus will be present. Even if we don't have Baba there physically, we will carry his spiritual blessings with us to Haridwar. Jai Jagannath! May everyone stay well and healthy. Let us take the Sanatanis forward. Let all Sanatanis unite and move forward; India will show the way to the world. India will again take the highest seat in the world assembly. That day will be great; that day will be great. <h3 style="color:var(--accent-bright);margin-top:0;">Spiritual Insight</h3>
<p>A short formal occasion, recorded almost without commentary: an organisation's president arrives with a delegation, speaks the language of <a href="/articles/topics/guru-stories-devotee-lives/">shraddha and sharanagati</a>, and asks for the guru's hand on his head. What gives the exchange its weight is Gurudev's reply - not a doctrine but a refusal to take credit, since the thing he says he wanted for years was for them to "set foot on the soil of West Bengal," and now that they have, he thanks God and Jagannath rather than himself. His blessing is then specific and modest: the mountains still stand, the rivers have not dried, "if we try, it will happen," and the credit is assigned to "the grace of Mother Tara."
