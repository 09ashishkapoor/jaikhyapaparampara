<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Pranam, Gurudev. Guru Purnima was just a few days ago. All the disciples remembered their gurus. But Gurudev, what is the way to distinguish between a real guru and a fake one?</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">That is a very significant question. You have to search through many pebbles to find where Narayana resides. When we search for a Shaligram Shila in the Gandaki River, we sift through countless stones. Out of a hundred thousand, perhaps one is found. The same applies to a guru. You must test him at every moment to see if he is honest or dishonest. 

You should observe him before taking initiation (Diksha). I often hear people say after initiation, "My guru knows nothing, can I take it again?" But once you are initiated, you cannot be initiated again. Most people are being deceived, often because of their own character. If you are a drunkard, you will look for a guru who drinks. A thief will look for a guru who can give him a mantra to break locks. You find a guru according to your own taste and nature. If your character is bad, you won't find a Sath-Guru (true guru).</span>

<h2>The Eight Markers of 'Sath' (Purity)</h2>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">There are eight markers of the divine, and all of them are "Sath" (pure/true). One who possesses these is a Sath-Guru:

1.  Sath-Karma (Pure Deeds): The work the guru does must be pure. Performing deeds just for money is dishonest. A true guru helps without taking fees and treats everyone equally.
2.  Sath-Drishti (Pure Vision): One’s vision must be pure. A guru must look at everyone with an equal eye and avoid Ku-drishti (evil eye). If a woman comes, he should see her as a mother.
3.  Sath-Vakya (Pure Speech): The words spoken must be truthful and not deceitful. One should not use the same mouth for profanities and chanting the name of the Lord.
4.  Sath-Jeevika (Pure Livelihood): The way of earning a living must be honest. Cheating, taking bribes, or instigating others are not signs of a pure livelihood.
5.  Sath-Prajatna (Pure Effort): Whatever is done must be done with care, sincerity, and mindfulness. It involves doing things secretly and with caution (Gopaniyam prajatnena).
6.  Sath-Smriti (Pure Memory): Our memory should be focused on purity, not on dishonest or harmful thoughts from the past.
7.  Sath-Samadhi (Pure Meditation): Meditation must be genuine, not a performance or a fake state entered when others are watching.
8.  Om Tat Sat (Ultimate Honesty): As the Gita says at the end of every chapter, "Om Tat Sat" means "You be honest." It is the final word on spiritual life.</span>
</div>

<h2>The Value of Initiation</h2>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">A true guru won't take money for initiation because God cannot be sold. The Beej Mantras (seed mantras) are what make everything work; without the seed, there is no fruit. We cannot sell that seed. We say "Kanchanamulyam Haritakiphalam"—the value of a simple Haritaki fruit is equal to gold when given in devotion. If you find these divine qualities in a human through observation—not by being swayed by flashy talk—then you should make him your guru.</span>
</div>

<h2>Hypnosis and 'Rakshas-kal'</h2>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Some people use Sambohan Shakti (hypnosis), which is associated with Ma Bagalamukhi. It shouldn't be used for subjugation (Vashikaran). I tell people clearly: I don't know it, and my guru didn't teach it.

Also, be aware of Rakshas-kal (demonic time). In our context, this is from 12:00 PM to 2:30 PM. This is the time when the "demon" within the human wakes up to eat. During this period, one should not perform Japa, worship, or rituals. Even the flowers we use should be pure; avoid hybrid flowers (like artificially created white or yellow hibiscus) for the Mother Goddess.</span>
</div>
