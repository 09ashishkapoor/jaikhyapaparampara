<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom: 20px;"><span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight: bold; color: #333;">Interviewer 1:</span> <span class="speech-text">The Birla Temple near Park Circus in Kolkata?</span></div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom: 20px;"><span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight: bold; color: #333;">Debjani Haldar:</span> <span class="speech-text">An extraordinary thing happened during that time—there was a lot of turmoil and unrest. Ashok Singhal ji was practically banned from entering Uttar Pradesh. He took Mr. Sompura with him, both in disguise. To design such a massive temple, they needed to measure the land. Mr. Sompura later recounted that he measured the site with his footsteps. He walked across those several acres of land, and based on the number of steps he took, the initial drawing of the temple was made. That design was later refined and expanded, but the core design remained based on those footsteps.</span></div>
<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom: 20px;"><span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight: bold; color: #333;">Interviewer 1:</span> <span class="speech-text">We haven't seen the final design yet, though some parts appeared in the media. <div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom: 20px;"><span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight: bold; color: #333;">Debjani Haldar:</span> <span class="speech-text">As far as I know, many motifs were found during the excavations that are symbolic of the Hindu religion. Many artifacts were unearthed. When the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) conducted excavations in 2003, they divided the area into several trenches. They found a large number of motifs and about 55 pillars from an ancient temple. It was evident that there was a vast level of pillars upon which something else was later built. That later structure had no foundation of its own; it was simply placed on top of the ancient temple's structure.</span></div>
<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom: 20px;"><span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight: bold; color: #333;">Interviewer 1:</span> <span class="speech-text">So, are the excavated motifs being used again?</span></div>

One remarkable find was a Makarmukha (crocodile-shaped spout), which is a North Indian style used for pouring water over Shiva's head. This was used as proof that a temple existed there. There were repeated claims that the site wasn't ancient or that it belonged to another religion. Some even said it was related to Buddhism because Ayodhya was known as Saket during Buddha's time.</span></div>
<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom: 20px;"><span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight: bold; color: #333;">Interviewer 1:</span> <span class="speech-text">But Buddha came long after Ram.</span></div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom: 20px;"><span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight: bold; color: #333;">Debjani Haldar:</span> <span class="speech-text">Another interesting detail involves a "Time Capsule." <div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom: 20px;"><span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight: bold; color: #333;">Debjani Haldar:</span> <span class="speech-text">Regarding the Buddhist claims, when motifs like the Kalash (urn) and lotus were found, some historians claimed they were Buddhist. <div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom: 20px;"><span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight: bold; color: #333;">Interviewer 1:</span> <span class="speech-text">Gurudev mentioned that a temple or structure doesn't always rise immediately at a birth site. <div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom: 20px;"><span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight: bold; color: #333;">Debjani Haldar:</span> <span class="speech-text">I'd like to clarify one more thing. Usually, even today, if we go to Silaidaha or Kamarpukur, our natural instinct is to see where Ramakrishna or Vivekananda were born. We touch the soil and consider it a holy pilgrimage site. Similarly, the site called Ram Janmabhoomi was known in Hindi as "Ram Kot," meaning Ram's fort or palace. An Austrian priest and traveler named Joseph Tieffenthaler visited Ayodhya in the mid-18th century (around 1740-1780). He was a linguist and wrote his first book in Latin, which was later translated into French, German, and finally English in 1822. In his writings, he used the word "Cradle" to describe the site where people would go to offer prayers. This term was later used in court to prove that Hindus had always worshipped there as the birthplace of Ram.</span></div>
