That is why she is called Katyayani, the presiding goddess of Katyayan's ashram.</span>

Thus, another of the Mother's names is Narayani.</span>

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">"Kola Bou" isn't a person; it represents a botanical aspect. Each plant represents a different goddess.</span>

The Ashoka plant represents the removal of grief (Shoka Rahita). Finally, the Banana tree itself.</span>
</div>

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">These nine plants are tied together to represent the Mother as "Naba Patrika Bashini Durga" (Durga residing in nine leaves). </div>

<h3 style="color:var(--accent-bright);margin-top:0;">Spiritual Insight</h3>
<p>Guru Shyama Khyapa shares profound esoteric knowledge about Mother Durga's true identity. He reveals her original name "Duggi" and her four primary manifestations: Katyayani (daughter of sage Katyayan), Dakshayani (daughter of Daksha), Narayani (who held Narayan in her womb), and Shibani (consort of Shiva). The teaching explains the botanical significance of Kola Bou—how nine medicinal plants represent different deities and the cosmic cycle of creation, preservation, and destruction. Most remarkably, he clarifies that Durga is actually childless (Muktakeshi), and the figures surrounding her represent cosmic principles rather than biological offspring.</p>
