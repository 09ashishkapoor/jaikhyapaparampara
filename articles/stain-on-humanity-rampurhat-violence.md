<h2>The Value of a Human Life</h2>

We who contemplate God know that humans are themselves manifestations of the Divine. When humans suffer, we must speak. Whether eight died or ten, the count doesn't matter as much as the fact that lives were extinguished. Today, we feel no security; we live in fear of the next bomb or the next bullet. We see news of ammunition being found everywhere—in Sagar, in Berhampore—and it is not a good sign for our future.

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Providing a job or a sum of money to "hush up" the matter is not justice. It is an attempt to cover up a deep sin. We see this destructive tendency everywhere—even globally, like what Russia did to Ukraine, ruining a nation's future for decades. We are witnessing a tendency to destroy whatever is good.</span>

<h2>The Call for Divine and Earthly Justice</h2>

As the great Kazi Nazrul Islam said: *"Are they Hindu or Muslim? Who asks that? Say, 'A human is drowning—the child of my mother!'"*

It doesn't matter if the victims were Hindu or Muslim; they were children of mothers. They were humans. To stay silent while such things happen is a pang in the conscience that lasts forever. We who call upon the Divine cannot accept this. We want the investigation—whether by the CBI or any central agency—to be completely independent and find the real culprits.
