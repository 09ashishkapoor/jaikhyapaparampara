<p class="transcript-context">Guru Shyama Khyapa tells how he came to sit at Rajpur cremation ground, beginning with the years he spent performing Kali sadhana in a six-by-six basement room while keeping an association's accounts. He describes the office-bearers' meeting that nearly ended the little shrine and instead had it rebuilt in stone, a sadhu's offhand instruction at the Narmada that opened into a vision of Mother Jagadhatri, and the thousand-day Maha Mrityunjaya Yagna that first gave him ground at the cremation ground. He also notes visitors from Croatia and Germany who came in recent weeks.</p>

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Look, I have performed puja in many places. Wherever I sat, it became a place of worship. <span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">The association told me, "Since you don't have much work right now..." By then, I had already practiced Kali sadhana quite a bit. "You don't have any problem, so sit here and look after the ledgers, sales tax, income tax, etc., for our association members." I said, "Alright, I'll do it." Doing it meant working in a basement room, which was about 6 feet by 8 feet, or maybe 6 by 6. I would check people's ledgers and also practice sadhana for the Mother. If anyone called, I wouldn't be found. But I was called. I had to go. They asked, "What have you done in the basement?" <h3 style="color:var(--accent-bright);margin-top:0;">Spiritual Insight</h3>
<p>Gurudev tells the story as a single unbroken line: a young man worshipping Kali quietly in a six-by-six basement room while auditing other people's ledgers, a committee that came to inspect the shrine and instead paid to rebuild it in stone, a sadhu's offhand instruction at the Narmada that opened into a vision of Mother Jagadhatri, and finally the thousand-day Maha Mrityunjaya Yagna at Rajpur Mahashmashan where Shantikunj now stands. In his telling the Mother arranges the rooms and the opponents become the donors; his part is only to keep the practice and not argue. The wish he made there - "let the whole world know about Rajpur Shmashan" - is the one he answers at the end with two visitors from Croatia and Germany asking for a stick to keep in a museum. Read it as testimony rather than biography, and as a teaching on patience in <a href="/articles/topics/sadhana-tantra-mantra/">sadhana</a>: the place was given, not seized.
