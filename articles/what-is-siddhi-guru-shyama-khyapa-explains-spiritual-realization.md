<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Guru Dev, three qualities are present in humans. At times, the quality of 'Sattva' (goodness/purity) resides, at other times 'Tamas' (darkness/ignorance), and 'Rajas' (passion/activity).</span>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">This applies to the Gods too.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Devotee:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Most people are driven by either 'Tamas' or 'Rajas'. Could you please explain these three qualities to us?</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">'Rajas' and 'Tamas'. 'Rajas' is the quality of passion. 'Tamas' is darkness, it’s shrouded in ignorance. We believe that Mahadev possesses the quality of 'Sattva'. Narayana has the quality of 'Tamas', and Brahma has the quality of 'Rajas'.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Brahma possesses the quality of 'Rajas'. Narayana is associated with 'Tamas', and Devadidev Mahadev is associated with 'Sattva'. These are the three qualities humans possess, though they are not as distinct as they are in the gods.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">In humans, these qualities are mixed. However, most people are driven by 'Rajas' and 'Tamas'. 'Rajas' is the quality of passion, whereas 'Tamas' is associated with a downward-looking mind and all kinds of bad deeds.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">'Rajas' can also be seen in religious activities, like playing the drums and feeding people. 'Sattva' is a state of being where one is free from everything. It is a clear state of being where there is no corruption. Everything in this state is in the realm of truth. This is what I know.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Devotee:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">We have seen people like Gautam Buddha, who were born into royal families and led luxurious lives. This means they were full of 'Rajas' and 'Tamas'. But then, they transitioned into the state of 'Sattva'. How did they achieve this transformation?</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">It’s not just a transformation, it was pre-planned. It was the fruit of their past lives. Regardless of whether they were born into a royal family or not, they would have achieved it. They were destined to achieve what they did, no matter where they were born.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Take the case of Prahlad. He was born into a royal family but had to endure a lot of hardship. In the end, he was saved by Narasimha Avatar. Similarly, Buddha was born into a royal family but he gradually gave himself to the people.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Devotee:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Some people want to be famous and want their names to be known. But there's no connection between fame and spiritual realization (Siddhi). We call some people famous, while we say others have attained spiritual realization. Could you please explain what it means to attain Siddhi?</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Siddhi is a state of being that you attain through the grace of God. Ashtasiddhi is one of the highest forms of Siddhi. It resides in the feet of great souls. Great souls can tell if someone has attained Ashtasiddhi just by looking at their feet. Siddhi means that whatever you say will come to pass.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Take the case of Shirdi Sai Baba. He attained Siddhi at the young age of 16. People like drunks and drug addicts would come to him. One day, they ran out of kerosene and the lamp went out. When they asked him what to do, he gave them some water and told them to put it in the lamp. The lamp started burning with the water and stayed lit all night. This is Siddhi. Whatever you want will happen.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Devotee:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">You often say that Siddhi cannot be given to anyone, but it spreads among people and one attains it eventually.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">It’s about doing good for people. Doing good for people is doing good for God. As you continue to do good, you will develop a power which is a part of Siddhi. You may not even realize it, but you will attain Siddhi.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Some people use their spiritual powers for bad purposes. They are driven by the quality of 'Tamas'. On the other hand, some people use their powers for the good of others. We use the same mouth to speak ill of others and to chant the name of God.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Can a common person's life change gradually as they decide to follow the path of spiritual practice? They may not become 'Sattva' overnight, but through long practice and endurance, can they achieve it?</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">It’s not easy. One has to endure a lot of hardship. People will harass you. The same people you are trying to help will harass you. But through their harassment, you will attain Siddhi. The same people who harassed you will eventually come to you and say that you have attained Siddhi. This is what happened with Shirdi Sai Baba.</span>

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">When he was 15 or 16, he was surrounded by drunks and drug addicts who would harass him. But gradually he attained Siddhi. <div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">They threatened to kill me, but I didn't pay any attention to them. I was focused on God and he protected me time and again. <span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Another one of your disciples, Bamanando Khepa, has also attained Siddhi. <div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Yes, it's common. Sometimes, they lose their temper and then they become calm again. It's a part of who they are. <div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
