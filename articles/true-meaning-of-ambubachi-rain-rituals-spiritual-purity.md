<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Who told you such nonsense? These are all lies. I was at Kamakhya for many years; the water remains as it is. These are rumors used to make money. 

The term Ambubachi has a beautiful meaning. 'Ambu' means water, and 'Bachan' means to observe or preserve. To preserve water is called Ambubachi. This word was used by the poet Michael Madhusudan Dutt: "Ambu-bimbo, Ambu-mukhe sattva-paati." In Tantra, we collect the rainwater that falls on the first day of the month of Ashar. Mother cannot be bathed directly during these days, so a bowl is placed in front of her; her reflection falls into the rainwater, and she is bathed symbolically.</span>
</div>

<h2>Respecting Mother Earth</h2>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Ambubachi is the time when the Earth is said to be menstruating. It is a period of intense heat where the first rains allow seeds to sprout. This is why plowing fields is strictly forbidden during these three days; farmers know that digging would destroy the seeds. No harm should be done to Mother Earth.

Similarly, Mother Kali in the temple should not be disturbed. We stop all Kirtan and loud chanting. Just as a woman requires peace and rest during her cycle, we let Mother be. We offer only milk and mangoes—which are like nectar and can neutralize toxins—pushed carefully under the temple door. It is a time for quiet respect, not for noisy rituals.</span>
</div>

<h2>Debunking the Commercialization of Faith</h2>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">At Kamakhya, millions are exchanged. People sell red cloths claiming they are stained by the Mother. It is a disaster. Think about the filth in those minds! They sell these fragments for 50 or 100 rupees, telling ordinary people their one-story house will become seven stories. It's all nonsense. 

A wise priest at Kamakhya once told me: "Never take anything home from the temple or the cremation ground during Ambubachi. If you do, your house will be destroyed." I followed that strictly. Real practice is about intelligence and science, not about falling for such scams.</span>
</div>
