They worship you like a god, or equivalent to God. I've seen people from Siliguri and other places who treat you as a deity. What do you have to say about this?</span>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">

Books are being published, and soon a movie might come out. This is not appropriate for a seeker (Sadhaka). A Sadhaka's true form emerges after death. After a Sadhaka passes away, their name spreads, and many things happen.</span>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">But now, I have to hear this repeatedly. Whoever comes says, "You are God. Just say 'yes' once, and I will leave. I won't leave until you say yes." I tell them, "Look, previously I might have said, 'Go, I will look after you.' But now I cannot say that." I have reached a certain spiritual stage where I shouldn't say such things. I used to say those things in an unconscious state during my earlier practice period. Now that period is over. People say these things, but it is harmful to my soul.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">People worship me as God, and while I am alive, this is not right. Yet, people still do it. </div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Regarding people touching my feet—I have allowed it. Everyone comes and bows at my feet. I allow it because the "Eight Siddhis" (supernatural powers) reside in the feet. When they touch my feet, they receive some benefit. Many gurus don't let people touch their feet, but my view is different. I believe my body was created by God for the people. My feet are for the people. So why should I hide them or prevent people from touching them?</span>

These things can be harmful to the soul because the soul needs to ascend through further stages. I have surpassed certain stages, but I still feel like I am wandering empty-handed. Ma hasn't given me anything yet that I can use to truly help people. I don't know what is happening behind the scenes, but people think I am God.</span>

<h3 style="color:var(--accent-bright);margin-top:0;">Spiritual Insight</h3>
<p>This profound conversation reveals the paradox of the Guru-disciple relationship. Gurudev repeatedly denies being God, emphasizing his own spiritual emptiness and the dangers of <strong>pride</strong> that come from being worshipped while alive. Yet he accepts the <strong>"tower and sub-station" analogy</strong>—that God doesn't come directly to devotees, but works through intermediaries like him. His humility is striking: at 76, he says his only regret is that "Ma hasn't given me anything." Yet millions are transformed by his presence. This reveals a profound truth about <strong>immortality</strong>: it is not physical deathlessness, but the lasting impact one leaves on others. As he quotes Bhabapagla: <em>"Man becomes God, man becomes an Avatar. It is people who make someone a deity... That is immortality."</em></p>
