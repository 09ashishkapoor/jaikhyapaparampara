<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Greetings, my son. Speak.</span>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Devotee:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Guru, today I would like to know about a specific subject. There are so many religions—Hindu, Parsi, Christian, Buddhist, Muslim. When a person from each of these religions dies, there are different customs. For example, Hindus cremate the body, Muslims bury it, and Parsis offer it to vultures. I want to understand from you—why is this?</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Look, this is the final stage. Death is the final chapter for a human being. There is nothing after this. The body is destroyed, but the soul does not perish. It remains and wanders around, trying to find a new body. It looks for a womb to enter. There are many aspects to this.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Now, our custom is to cremate. Parsis offer the body to vultures and kites; they leave the deceased on a rooftop, and the birds consume it. Muslims bury the body. These are the three main practices—burial, Parsi customs (sometimes on mountains where birds eat them), and cremation.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">We need to look at what is scientific. Human beings are born, but they must also die. What should be done with this perishable body? We, the followers of the Sanatan Hindu Dharma, decided on cremation. No germs remain, nothing is left. For instance, when you bury a body in the earth, germs form inside the grave within the body. In the soil, these germs grow and eventually find a way out. But in our case, there is no such issue. We burn it, and it turns into ash.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Devotee:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">From the five elements it came, and to the five elements it returns.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Yes. As the poet Ramprasad sang: "I have brought nothing with me, whatever I earned through hard work, the five elements consume." These five elements are: Earth, Water, Fire, Air, and Ether (Space). These five elements make up our body. When we enter a womb, these five elements immediately begin forming the body. That is why Ramprasad said that the five elements consume everything. Therefore, the Hindu Sanatan Dharma gives this preference. Once burned, nothing of this perishable body remains; it turns into ashes. No matter how large the body, after cremation, the ashes weigh about one and a half kilograms. This is well-proven. We used to use the old measurement of "Seer," which is roughly equivalent to a kilogram today.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">After death, the real transition to the afterlife begins. From the day of death, the rituals for the afterlife start. We wear the sacred thread or observe specific mourning rituals. We control our food, sleep, and conduct. For a Brahmin, this lasts 13 days; for others, it is one month. Not everyone is a Brahmin, so others observe a month of "Ashouch" (ritual impurity). This is the tradition.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">I remember a gentleman once asked a mourner, "Have you observed the full month?" The man replied, "No, we did it for 15 days, like the Brahmins." The gentleman said, "No, go back and observe the full month; I will provide whatever money is needed." I forget his name now, but he was quite famous. He insisted that he wouldn't touch them unless they completed the full month of mourning.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Now, what does the ritual for the afterlife mean? It is very scientific. Previously, we used wood for cremation, though now electric furnaces are common. Even in electric crematoriums, the custom is to place a piece of wood on the chest. Without wood, the body is not considered ritually purified.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">When the funeral rites (Shraddha) are performed, who receives the offerings first? Those who "burned" with me—the insects and germs that were on or in the body. They receive the "Pindo" (offering) first. During cremation, no family members are "with" me in the fire. Only the organisms that burn with the body are there. The mantra says: "Those beings who were consumed by the fire along with my body, who were part of my lineage at that moment, let them be satisfied."</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">We have three lineages: the father’s, the mother’s, and the maternal uncle’s. At the time of cremation, everyone else stays back out of fear or custom, but these organisms stay and perish with the body. That is why they are the first to receive the offering. Look at how scientific the Shraddha ritual is! They receive the offering first, and then the "Pretapindo" is given to the deceased. Most people don't know these details—99% of people are unaware. Priests know because they perform the rituals.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Furthermore, the soul remains in the house for ten days. It doesn't want to leave. It stays, but in great distress, because although it sees its loved ones, no one can talk to it.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Devotee:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Loved ones are walking right by, but the soul is thirsty and no one gives it water.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Exactly. It has no voice, no physical body. It wants water or something else, but it cannot communicate, and no one speaks to it. This is a very painful time for the soul (Preta)—spending those ten days in the house. It follows you back from the crematorium. You might think ten people went and only nine came back, but the deceased also returns and stays for ten days. After ten days, there is a ritual at the riverbank where ten "Pindos" are offered. Since the soul hasn't eaten for ten days, a full day's worth of food is provided, and it is ritually requested to leave the house and move on.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Devotee:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">But Guru, even after cremation, I see people placing sweets or water in front of a photograph.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">That is nonsense. It’s useless. One shouldn't even keep a photo of the deceased in that manner; I believe it can be harmful. Regardless, one should never place food or water in front of the photo.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Guru, what about the "Sati" practice during the British period?</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">That was a social evil, a superstition.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Devotee:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Was it sanctioned by the scriptures?</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">No, no, not at all. It was a superstition. There were many such social evils back then. They believed if a husband died, the wife had to die with him. She had no right to live.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Devotee:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">So, there was no scriptural injunction?</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">None. It was just a few greedy and cruel people who created these rules. It was horrific—burning a living woman with her dead husband while playing loud drums to drown out her screams. Raja Ram Mohan Roy and Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar fought against this. They couldn't accept it. Ram Mohan Roy, who founded the Asiatic Society, studied in Varanasi and proved through scriptures that widow remarriage was permissible and Sati was not required.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Back then, people were very superstitious. For example, if someone crossed the ocean, it was called "Kala Pani" (Black Water), and they were outcasted from society. Michael Madhusudan Dutt, Swami Vivekananda, Rabindranath Tagore—they all faced social backlash or had to pay penalties for traveling abroad. Raja Ram Mohan Roy also went. It was a social rule: cross the sea, and you are outcasted.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Devotee:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">What was the reason for that?</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">It was just a rule made by the pundits of that time. There was no scriptural basis. It was like how a Brahmin wouldn't let a "Methor" (sweeper) come within ten feet of him to avoid their shadow. Yet, if that same person gave him money, he would take it, wash it with water from his pot, and put it in his pocket. These were the double standards of the time.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Anyway, we are drifting. On the tenth day, the "Ghat Pindo" is given. The soul is told, "You haven't eaten for ten days, eat now and leave the house." From that moment, the soul begins searching for a new womb. It prefers to stay within its own family if a womb is available; if not, it looks elsewhere. It’s like being in a foreign land—if you find someone who speaks your language, you feel a sense of belonging. The soul tries to stay among its own people. When it can’t find a place there, it goes wherever it can. This is our Hindu philosophy.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Devotee:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Guru, during the one-year mourning period ("Kaal-Ashouch"), can the soul take a new body?</span>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Yes, birth can happen within a year.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Devotee:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Then why is the "Pindo" given after one year?</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">It is a tradition, a scriptural custom. You observe it for a year until the soul is reborn. Usually, a year is the standard wait. Also, the "Mukh-Agni" (the fire touched to the mouth during cremation) must be ritually cleared. A year later, a Shraddha is performed, often called the "Bantsharik" (Annual Rite). It can be done all at once or monthly. It’s a long process. A good priest will take until evening to finish. After that, the fire ritual is concluded. Until then, the "shadow" of that fire remains, and we believe God does not accept offerings from that person. That is why the period of mourning is observed—to remember the deceased and allow for the soul's transition. Usually, after ten months and ten days, a soul is reborn and becomes "free" from its previous state.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Devotee:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Guru, we never knew these things. We never even tried to understand.</span>
