<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">

</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">That Mahaprasad is also used for children's Annaprashan (first rice ceremony). Many things can be done with it. That Mahaprasad is powerful. Everything can be accomplished through it.</span>

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">That dried rice is the Lord's prasad. We feed that Mahaprasad if a dog or something bites. Not for snake bites, but specifically for dog bites, we feed it.</span>

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Mahaprasad is no trivial matter. It is always salt-free; there is no salt in Mahaprasad. In fact, no sacred offering contains salt; they are all salt-free.</span>

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Lord Jagannath eats everything without salt. Now, Balram has different items, and Jagannath has different ones. <span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Coarse rice and coarse cloth for Jagannath. He loves to eat tubers and earth-grown vegetables. That's what is cooked for him—wild potatoes, tubers, and various leafy vegetables.</span>
