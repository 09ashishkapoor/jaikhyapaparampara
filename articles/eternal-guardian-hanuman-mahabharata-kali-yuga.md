<h2>Signs of the Falling Flag: Jagannath Puri</h2>

When a flag falls, it signifies the defeat or departure of a king. Recently, the <i>Garuda Dhvaja</i> of the Jagannath Temple fell—a symbolic sign of the <i>Pralaya</i> (dissolution) mentioned in the <b>Bhabishya Malika</b>. The shedding of blood within the temple and other unprecedented events indicate that the Lord has left the premises, leaving the spirit of Kali Yuga in His place. These are the markers of the transition toward the Kalki Avatar.

<h2>The Heart of Krishna and the Daru of Puri</h2>

The end of Lord Krishna’s physical journey was tragic. After the Yadu dynasty was destroyed by Gandhari's curse, his heart would not burn in the funeral pyre. Thrown into the sea, it floated to the shores of Puri. That divine heart became the <i>Daru</i> (sacred log) from which the icons of Lord Jagannath are carved. It is this same sanctity that is now under threat as the age of Kali peaks.

<h2>Seeking the Immortal</h2>

While we recite the <i>Hanuman Chalisa</i>, a direct vision of Bajrangbali is nearly impossible in this corrupted age. Saint Tulsidas was the last widely known devotee to have his <i>Darshan</i>. Immortal beings like Hanuman and Parashurama remain as silent witnesses to the unfolding of time. God does not want your wealth; He wants your devotion. To reach Him, one must leave material greed behind and become a true <i>Sadhak</i>.
