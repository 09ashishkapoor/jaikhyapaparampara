**Follower:** Gurudev, salutations. There is a saying that the crematorium "calls out" at night. Have you ever heard it?

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">I have felt it. In my 42 years in the crematorium, I have heard that call. It is a terrifying, irresistible call. At night, the crematorium stays alone and wants a friend—a *Shashan-bondhu*. We, the *Sadhaks*, are those friends. It calls, "Come, come, come," and you must go.</span>
</div>

I used to leave home at 10:00 PM on a broken bicycle to answer that call. Not everyone can hear it; you need "special ears." Every crematorium must have a Neem tree. It is antiseptic, but there is a deeper meaning. I've seen the Neem tree here "die" from someone's rituals and then come back to life after prayers.

In *Tantra*, we wait for specific moments—like when the body of a *Sadhoba* (a married woman) or a child arrives. The character of the crematorium changes instantly. The place is protected by *Shashan Bhairav* and invisible guards. When I used to walk home, two guards with sticks and loincloths would walk me to my door and then vanish.

**Follower:** How did you keep the crematorium awake?

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">My mere presence keeps it awake. If the *Sadhak* sleeps, the Mother sleeps. I've spent nights under Haritaki trees, where *Brahmadaityas* reside. At dawn, you can hear the sound of Lord Narayan's wooden sandals—*khatas khatas*—as He goes for a bath.</span>
</div>

Once, I had no money for tea or cigarettes. I thought of my disciple Prabir. Suddenly, he arrived on his cycle. He said someone had nudged him three times while he slept and told him to go to the crematorium. That is the power of the call. Everything I say is the truth of the Shashan.
