<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">We keep the crematorium awake. For example, I might leave the crematorium at 10:00 PM to go home and eat. Upon reaching home, I find nothing has been cooked. So, I turn back.</span>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:1.5rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Follower:</span>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:1.5rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">I've heard that too. But whether that's the real reason or if it's because of spirits (Bhoot-Pret), it's hard to say.</span>

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">The idol of Lord Jagannath in Puri is made from Neem wood. We find a link there. The Neem tree at the entrance of this crematorium has died two or three times because of such acts.</span>

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">People practiced on the tree as a rehearsal or a test. It appeared completely dead three times. You might say it's for antiseptic reasons, but there is a deeper, hidden meaning that we still haven't fully grasped.</span>
</div>
