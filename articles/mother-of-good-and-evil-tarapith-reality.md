<h2>The Nature of Mother Tara</h2>

We often confuse Mother Tara with a human mother. She is not a mother born of a womb; she is a cosmic force. She does not "grieve" if people die in accidents or suffer. If you lie in her temple asking for water, she won't hand it to you. She is impartial and detached. To say she is the "mother of both the good and the bad" as *Ma Sarada* did is true in a cosmic sense, but it doesn't mean she condones evil. No mother can truly tolerate the badness of her children when it causes pain.

<h2>The Danger of the Shmashan</h2>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Tarapith is a massive cremation ground (*Shmashan*). Lighting a fire there is not just a ritual; it is like lighting a funeral pyre. When people "deposit" names and lineages (*Gotra*) into that ground for ill intent, it has serious consequences. The ground is "awakened" and hungry. I have seen countless examples of people suffering Agonizing pain or facing accidents because their names were misused in that cemetery.</span>
</div>

<h2>Kaushiki Amavasya: Not for Householders</h2>

In my opinion, householders (*Grihis*) should avoid Tarapith during *Kaushiki Amavasya*. That day is meant for serious Tantriks and saints. For a common person, the spiritual "heat" and the prevalence of improper rituals can be harmful. I have personally suffered for over a month after helping someone counteract a dark ritual from Tarapith—even when my intent was purely for their welfare.
