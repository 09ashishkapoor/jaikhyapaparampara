</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Now, in our East, we have Indra, the lord of the thunderbolt (Vajra). He is the lord of storms. <div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">The mantra for the East is: "Lam Indraya Suradhipataye Pitavarnaya Vajrahastaya Airavatavahanaya Saparivaraya Namah." </div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">The East is protected by Indra. Now, after East, let's come to the West. The West is protected by Varuna, the god of water. Varuna Dev is seated on a Makara (a sea creature). The mantra is: "Vam Varunaya Jaladhipataye Pashahastaya Ankushavahanaya Saparivaraya Namah."</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">"Vam Varunaya Jaladhipataye Shuklavarnaya Pashahastaya Makaravahanaya Saparivaraya Namah." The Makara refers to the crocodile; he sits on a crocodile. This is his mantra. Offerings are made with flowers.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">After East and West, let's go to the North. In the North is Kubera, the lord of the Yakshas. We say: "Kam Kuberaya Yakshadhipataye Naravahanaya Gadahastaya Saparivaraya Namah." So, Kubera is in the North.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">We often talk about Gandhal (a place). That is Kubera's city, the city of Yakshas. It is in the North. That's why the North is so magnetic for us.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Then comes the South. The mantra for the North was: "Kam Kuberaya Yakshadhipataye Naravahanaya Gadahastaya Saparivaraya Namah." North is protected by Kubera.</span>
</div>

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Now, who protects the South? Yama. "Dam Yamaya." These sounds—Dam, Vam, Am, Ram, Lam—are all "Beej" (seed) mantras. For the North, the Beej was Kam. For the South, where Yama resides, it is Dam.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">"Dam Yamaya Pretadhipataye Krishnavarnaya Dandahastaya Mahishavahanaya Saparivaraya Namah." He sits on a Mahisha (buffalo). Many people get scared if they see a buffalo in their dreams because Yama is on a buffalo. Yama is in the South.</span>
</div>

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">"Dam Yamaya Pretadhipataye Krishnavarnaya Dandahastaya Mahishavahanaya Saparivaraya Namah."</span>

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">So, East, West, North, South are done. Now, Ishan (Northeast). In Ishan, it is said: "Ham Ishanaya Bhutadhipataye Shuklavarnaya Saparivaraya Namah." The Northeast corner is considered the haunt of ghosts and spirits (Bhuta-Preta). That's why one should not build a bathroom in the Northeast corner. No toilet chambers should be there because spirits reside there.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Next is Nairit (Southwest). "Kham Nairitaye Rakshasadhipataye Shuklavarnaya Pashahastaya Saparivaraya Namah."</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Then, after Ishan and Nairit, comes Agni (Southeast). "Ram Agnaye Tejodhipataye Raktavarnaya Saparivaraya Namah." Fire is there. That's why it is said to keep the kitchen in the Southeast (Agni) corner.</span>

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">After Agni, Ishan, and Nairit, there is the Vayu (Northwest) corner. Vayu (wind) provides us with "Pran" (life force). That's why windows should be placed in the Northwest corner so that the life-giving air can enter.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">The mantra for Vayu is: "Vam Vayave Pranadhipataye Mrigavahanaya Saparivaraya Namah." Vayu is the lord of life (Pranadhipati). And wind is swift, so he is seated on a deer (Mriga).</span>

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Next is Urdhva (Upward). "Am Brahmanne Prajadhipataye Namah." Lord Brahma, the creator, is above.</span>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>

<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">This is why weapons cannot strike from two directions—upward and downward. They can only strike from the other eight directions.</span>

<h3 style="color:var(--accent-bright);margin-top:0;">Spiritual Insight</h3>
<p>The ancient wisdom of the Dash Dik Pal (Ten Directional Guardians) reveals the profound science behind what we now call Vastu Shastra and Feng Shui. Gurudev explains that modern practices of home placement—kitchens in the Southeast (Agni), bathrooms away from the Northeast (Ishan), windows in the Northwest (Vayu)—all originate from the sacred mantras and guardians of the ten directions. Each direction has its ruling deity with specific seed mantras: Indra (East, Lam), Varuna (West, Vam), Kubera (North, Kam), Yama (South, Dam), and others. The article presents the complete mantras for all ten guardians and explains why these rituals are specifically performed during Durga Puja alongside the Ashta Shakti, Ashta Bhairava, and Choushatti Yogini. Gurudev also shares practical insights about "Masha Bhakti Bali"—the essential offerings to spirits before any ritual to prevent disturbances, based on his direct experiences at the cremation grounds.</p>
