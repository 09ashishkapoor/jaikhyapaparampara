<h3 style="color:var(--accent-bright);margin-top:0;">Spiritual Insight</h3>
<p>Guru Shyama Khyapa and disciple Shantanu Ray deliver a groundbreaking revelation about <strong>Kaal Sarp Dosh</strong>: it is <strong>not mentioned in Vedic astrology or the Puranas</strong>. This concept was entirely created by astrologers for business interests and financial gain. The term breaks down as: <strong>"Kaal" = time/moment</strong> and <strong>"Sarp" = snake/body</strong>. In biological terms, the snake represents the human digestive tract—a coiled tube from mouth to exit. In astrological terms, "Kaal Sarp" refers to the <strong>birth process itself</strong>: the time between when the baby's head emerges and when the full body is delivered. This is the "Kaal Sarp" moment—the duration of complete birth.</p>
<p>Since everyone is born this way, <strong>everyone has Kaal Sarp</strong>—Gurudev, Shantanu, the cameraman, all of us. It cannot be "removed" because that would require destroying the body itself. The Guru clarifies that if people feel compelled to do something, they can visit <strong>Trimbakeshwar in Nasik</strong>, bathe in the Godavari River, and pray to Lord Shiva. However, this is more for psychological peace than any actual "dosh" removal.</p>
<p>Shantanu Ray explains that in the Upanishads, we are all manifestations of <strong>Brahma (the Supreme Creator)</strong>—we are molecules of the Divine. The Roman symbol of a snake biting its own tail represents this cycle: birth and death are certain, and we consume our own time. Regarding the technical astrological definition (all planets between Rahu-Ketu axis), Shantanu clarifies that <strong>Rahu and Ketu are always opposite each other</strong>. If all planets are on one side, it doesn't indicate a curse—in fact, such horoscopes are often <strong>very strong and powerful</strong>, producing exceptionally talented individuals. The Guru announces that future sessions will debunk "Mangalik" and "Sade Sati" myths as well, emphasizing that many people have been cheated by astrologers using these fear-based concepts. The message is clear: study the Vedas, Puranas, Gita, and epics to understand the interconnected truth, and don't let superstition fool you.</p>
