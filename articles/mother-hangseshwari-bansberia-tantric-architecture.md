<h3 style="color:var(--accent-bright);margin-top:0;">Spiritual Insight</h3>
<p>The Hangseshwari Temple of Bansberia stands as one of West Bengal's most profound yet misunderstood spiritual treasures. Built by King Nrisingha Deb Ray—a yogi who spent five years mastering Tantra, Yoga, and the secrets of Kula-kundalini in Benares—this five-story temple mirrors human anatomy itself. The name reveals its essence: "Hang" represents Shiva (inhalation), "Sa" represents Shakti (exhalation), forming "Hangsa"—the breath that sustains life, residing in the throat. The temple's 13 towers (Gombuj), each crowned with a lotus, map the stages of spiritual realization that correspond to the Shat-chakras (six chakras) from Svadhisthana to Vishuddha. The Mother's idol, carved from rare blue neem wood, depicts her seated on a lotus emerging from Shiva's navel—holding sword, severed head, and offering Abhaya and Varada mudras. Begun in 1799 and completed by Queen Shankari in 1814 after the King's death, this architectural masterpiece embeds everything required for Tantric practice and mastery of the senses. Nrisingha Deb Ray refused even a trip to Britain to complete this "map of the body's internal energy"—a testament to his dedication that true seekers must visit to comprehend.</p>
