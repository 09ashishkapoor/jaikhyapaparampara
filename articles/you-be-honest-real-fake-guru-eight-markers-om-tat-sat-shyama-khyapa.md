<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Pranam, pranam. Tell me.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Devotee:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Gurudev, Guru Purnima was just a few days ago. All the disciples and followers remembered their gurus, worshipped them, and performed rituals. Gurudev, in this context, I had a question: what is the way to distinguish between a real guru and a fake one?</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">That is a very significant question. You see, you have to search through many pebbles to find where Narayana (the divine) resides. When we search for a Shaligram Shila in the Gandaki River, we have to sift through countless stones. Out of a hundred thousand pebbles, perhaps one Shaligram Shila is found. No doubt about it. That is why it is said, "One must look to see in which pebble Narayana resides." The same applies to a guru. You must test him. You must test him at every moment—not just in one way, but in various ways—to see if he is honest or dishonest. A true guru has a certain dignity, and a false guru has another. By observing and interacting, you can understand if he is genuine.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Devotee:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Should this testing be done before taking Diksha (initiation)?</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Of course! You should observe him before taking initiation. I often hear people say after initiation, "My guru knows nothing, I have already taken initiation; can I take it again?" They come to me and ask, "Can I take initiation from you?" I ask them why. Once you are initiated, you cannot be initiated again. According to our Hindu traditions, once a woman is married, she doesn't marry again—well, it happens nowadays, so let's leave that aside. Similarly, nowadays, people might have ten gurus or ten marriages. There's no point in arguing about that. However, most people are being deceived. Firstly, the kind of guru you find depends on your own character. If you are a drunkard, you will look for a guru who drinks; then both of you can drink together. That is the kind of guru you will seek. A thief will look for a guru who is a thief—one who can give him a mantra to break locks. This is the reality. One finds a guru according to their own taste and nature. Someone who smokes marijuana will look for a guru who can shatter a pipe in one puff. They'll say, "Look at my guru, he can clear a pipe in one go!" These things lead to confusion. You will find a guru according to your character. If your character is bad or if you are addicted to something, you will find a similar guru. You won't find a Sath-Guru (true/pure guru).</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Devotee:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">How do we recognize a Sath-Guru?</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Look, there are eight markers of the divine, and all of them are "Sath" (pure/true). Among them is Sath-Karma (pure deeds). The work you do, or the work the guru does, must be pure, not dishonest. If a guru performs deeds just to take money or belongings from people, that is not a good deed.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Devotee:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">You mean in exchange for money?</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Yes, doing things for money is not right. It is not a pure deed; it is dishonest. If you help someone without taking money or treat everyone equally without a fee, that is a sign of a true guru. A dishonest guru will ask for money immediately after helping you. But even that depends on your luck; you might end up with a dishonest guru because you have a greed for money or want a quick fix. "How much? Fifty thousand? Okay, take it."</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Devotee:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Some devotees show off the jingling of their money everywhere, even to the guru. Then there are some who are very poor.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Yes, the poor ones come and say right away, "Gurudev, I have nothing. Please treat me if you can, otherwise, let it be." They come knowing that this Gurudev doesn't take money. But some people come with their money, thinking they can buy the guru. We have different "rates" for different categories—I'm joking, of course. For a doctor, there's one rate; for a policeman, another; for a lawyer, another. It's not the same for everyone.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Devotee:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">So, it depends on the person and the place?</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">I ask them, "How much will you give?" They might say they don't have a single penny. I say, "Then come in." This shows partiality in one sense, but in truth, it's about Sath-Drishti (pure vision). One's vision must be pure. One must look at everyone with an equal eye. Not just that, but avoiding Ku-drishti (evil eye) or Ku-vakya (evil speech). If a woman comes, one shouldn't look at her with bad intentions. A lot depends on your gaze. Do you understand? Like winking or suggestive talk—that is a sign of an evil eye. Your gaze should be pure. If I look at a woman, I should see her as a mother. This is how it should be. The vision must be pure. Then, the words I speak to you should not be deceitful; they should be truthful. Sath-Vakya (pure speech). With this same mouth, I might utter profanities, and with the same mouth, I chant the name of the Lord. It's the same mouth. So, one must always remember that the words spoken must be pure. Sath-Vakya.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Next is Sath-Jeevika (pure livelihood). The way I earn my living must be honest. Using dishonest means or taking bribes is not a sign of a pure livelihood.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Devotee:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Like cheating people?</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Yes, cheating people, taking bribes, or instigating others—these are not signs of a pure livelihood. If someone has these traits, we cannot call them "Sath." Never.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Then there is Sath-Prajatna (pure effort). Whatever we do, we must do it with care and mindfulness. We say, "Effort must be made with caution." Whatever you do, you must maintain it with sincerity. Sath-Prajatna. "Gopaniyam prajatnena savadhana..." It means doing things secretly, with effort, and with caution. All three are mentioned. You must maintain this.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Then there is Smriti (memory). Our memory power is vast; memories from 50, 60, or 70 years ago are swirling within us. This memory should be pure, not dishonest. Sath-Smriti.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">And finally, there is Sath-Samadhi (pure meditation). For those who enter a state of Samadhi—perhaps while talking, or walking, or sitting—it must not be fake. You might see someone coming from a distance and pretend to enter Samadhi. That is not desirable. That too must be genuine.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">This is why we say, "Om Tat Sat." When you read the Gita, at the end of every chapter, it says "Om Tat Sat." What does it mean? It means "You be honest/pure." That is the final word. "Iti" means drawing a line—I've drawn the line, no more talk. Similarly, it is said: "You be honest." Every time you read a chapter of the Gita, it tells you: "Be honest, be honest. Speak the truth, be pure."</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Devotee:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Truth and sincerity.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Yes, truth and sincerity. These are the things being said. One who possesses these qualities can be considered divine. You can call him God. You can take initiation from him without hesitation. He won't give initiation for anything other than a Haritaki (a medicinal nut). He won't take money for initiation because God cannot be sold. The Beej Mantras (seed mantras) of God are what make everything work. Without a seed, there is no tree; without a tree, there is no fruit. So the seed is everything. We cannot sell that seed. Many people give initiation for 30,000 or 40,000 rupees; I know this, I've seen it. But we cannot do that. "Kanchanamulyam Haritakiphalam"—meaning the value of a Haritaki fruit is equal to the value of gold. That is what we say. During the time of Dakshina (offering), priests say this. So, a guru who has these qualities is a Sath-Guru. He is God. You can comfortably call him God if these eight markers are present in him. If you find these divine qualities in a human through observation and interaction—not by being impulsive or being swayed by flashy talk—then you should make him your guru.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Devotee:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Gurudev, in some places, it is seen that people get hypnotized.</span>

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Yes, that happens. I am not denying it.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Is this done through the guru's actions or rituals?</span>

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Look, there is no such mantra to subjugate you or make you speechless. At least, I don't know any, and I never do such things.</span>

I don't do it, and I tell people clearly. If someone asks me to subjugate someone for them...</span>

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Vashikaran (subjugation)?</span>

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Yes, Vashikaran. I say I can't do it. I don't know it. I haven't learned those mantras, and my guru didn't teach them to me.</span>

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Gurudev, what is meant by Rakshas-kal (demonic time)?</span>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Rakshas-kal? First, tell me, who are the Rakshasas (demons)? Humans are the Rakshasas. Humans are demons. Do you understand? When is the time for humans to eat? From 12:00 PM to 2:30 PM—that's when the "demons" eat. During this time, one should not perform Japa (chanting), worship, or any rituals. This is called Rakshas-kal.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Devotee:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">I see.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru Shyama Khyapa:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">At that time, the demon wakes up—the demon within the human. Ravana was a Rakshas, but in human form. Rakshasas in human form. So, that is the time for demons to eat. After working all day, they eat between 12:00 and 2:00. That's Rakshas-kal. However, this is more applicable here in West Bengal. In other places, it might be different. I've stayed in Delhi for a long time. In the morning, they might have two rotis, some vegetables, and a glass of milk, and then leave. They won't eat anything all day and then eat again at night. They used to tell me, "If you need to, go to Golpark, eat some fish, take a bath, and then come home." They followed that strictly. Anyway, that's a different story. Since we eat everything—fish, meat, eggs—we don't leave anything out. If cooked well, we might even eat snake meat or human meat!
