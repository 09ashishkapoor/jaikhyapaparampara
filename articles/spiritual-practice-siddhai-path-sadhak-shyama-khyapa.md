<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Look, one does not become a Sadhak just by calling themselves one. If one does not have the "Sukriti" (merit from good deeds) of previous births, they cannot become a Sadhak. No matter how much they practice, this is not a matter of a single lifetime. It is a matter of many lifetimes. Not only that, you don't just suddenly start practicing in one birth and see the Mother (the Divine Mother). This is a matter of many lifetimes. Buddha said this; Krishna told Arjuna that this is a matter of many lifetimes.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">So, it's not like I just set up an idol and start worshiping, or do something casually—that doesn't yield results. Those who come to me, who are practicing, tell me—many educated young men are trying to enter the realm of God, but entering the realm of God is a distant dream. They talk about various difficulties they face. And difficulties will surely arise; this is "Abhyas Yoga" (the yoga of practice). The main thing is that those who practice should initially stay in a confined room and avoid social contact with people.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">You won't attain "Siddhai" (spiritual perfection) in a single day. It takes a lot of time. A lot of time. Attaining "Siddhai" is not a small feat. You have to receive "Siddhai" over time; time will grant you that perfection. It is, you could say, a divine instruction—to "take Siddhai." Now, like us, we received "Siddhai" much later. At the time of receiving it, we had to perform "Hom Yagya" (sacrificial fire rituals) and many other things. Then we received it, and I received it sitting on a deer skin. You will see the photo of me on a full deer skin. You cannot take "Siddhai" just by saying so; it doesn't happen anytime. It happens at a very specific auspicious moment.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Guru:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">So, just as it is a matter of time, it is also a matter of God. Now, what are we doing? Along with our practice, we are trying to build a temple because we are looking at the future, and I am also looking at the financial aspect. But then it won't work. Nothing else should be considered here; here, it is only God and you. No second or third person can enter between you. Through constant practice, by awakening God, only then can you understand some indications that God gives. When you receive those indications, you will understand that you are moving on the right path.</span>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Many people, after a little worship and wearing saffron clothes, suddenly claim to have attained "Siddhai" and call themselves "Siddha Purush" (perfected beings). But one may or may not receive the internal tools and ingredients of Tantra practice. We, for example, have received them. We received tiger skin, upon which we performed worship. We received deer skin. Besides that, whatever is needed for Tantra practice, the Mother has provided everything. And through these provisions, a Sadhak understands that perhaps the time for "Siddhai" has come. Certain indications are found. Only after seeing those indications can you receive "Siddhai."</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Man does not grant "Siddhai"; God Himself grants it. After receiving it, when you perform work for the people and that work is done well by the Mother's instruction—understand that this won't happen without the Mother's command. Now, even if you want to do good for people, you won't be able to do it alone; it becomes an internal realization. To do that good—look at us, after how many years of practice are we working for the welfare of people? My goal was always there; I used to tell my Gurudev repeatedly, "Please give me a bit of your supernatural power so that I can show people something extraordinary." In that case, Gurudev granted me that much later. And today, the term "Gupta Sadhak" (secret seeker) means—do you know? I practiced secretly, so I am a "Gupta Sadhak." I never did it openly. Whatever I did, I did secretly. So, everyone knows me as a "Gupta Sadhak."</span>

"Gopanam Prayatnena Savdhana Padharaya"—it must be maintained in three ways: secretly, carefully, and with effort. If the Mother is satisfied with that, it is very good. Otherwise, it might take many lifetimes. It doesn't end in one birth; it's a matter of lifetimes. How many of my births have passed just calling out to God? I am doing good for people; look, I am doing good without taking anything. Perhaps I gave someone a small shell or some other item, and through that, the person gets better. Again, many people come and say that just touching me will make them better, and so on. For instance, today two people came from Guwahati, and one came from Siliguri and Cooch Behar. I saw many who, after a little practice, got busy with a temple and started collecting money. So, what happens then? Your freedom—the way you were calling God—gets hindered. You get busy with property. This happened in the case of Ramprasad too. When King Krishnachandra gave him some money and land, he said one thing: "Akhon tomar bishoy lagi, dangshiche mor joge jage" (Now, because of your property, it stings during my meditation). That is, whenever I try to perform rituals or practice, this property or money disturbs me. This was his realization, and he wrote it in his books. To perform rituals, you cannot have this money and such things. You have to do it by being completely detached. Detached from money, gold, or even women—they cannot be there. If they are there, various troubles will arise. It's called "Moni-Kanchon." So, women shouldn't be there either; their presence brings more danger. Many have gotten entangled and are struggling till the end. So, the only thing is, the goal should be clear—why have you come here? Have you come for the Mother, or have you come to build a temple and earn money? You have to think about this. If you earn money by showing the Mother, and build a temple with the Mother's money, your spiritual practice won't happen. A temple might be built, but there won't be any spiritual practice there, and the Mother won't be there either. The Mother wants to be awakened, she wants someone to talk to her, to sit with her in solitude. Many cannot do this; they get lost in the greed for money or in building temples and collecting funds. When will they practice? Practice is not a simple matter. It takes time, a lot of time. It's time-consuming. And if done methodically—look, after how many years did we receive...</span>

In those cases, we don't say no. We do the work, and they don't even get to know. If I work, even their ancestors won't know that I have worked. The work is done very secretly. Taking the name and lineage, the work is done secretly—what is called "Dharna" (pleading) before the Mother. That's the main thing.</span>
