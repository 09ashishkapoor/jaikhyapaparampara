<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Pranam, my son, Pranam.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Disciple:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Recently, we have noticed that many of your followers are eager to know about Markandeya Muni. If you could please shed some light on that subject.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Gurudev:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Haldar Saheb here can speak very well about Markandeya Rishi. I know a little, but he can explain it much better. Pranam, Pranam.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Haldar Saheb:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Pranam, Gurudev. To speak of Markandeya Muni, rather than just telling a simple story of his life, we should mention the connection to the thousand-day Mahamrityunjaya Yagna held at Shanti Kunj. I will start from there. We know that Markandeya Muni lived through seven Kalpas (aeons). This was by the grace and blessings of Lord Mahadev. He performed deep penance for Shiva, and Mahadev granted him the Mahamrityunjaya blessing.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Disciple:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">But first, we should mention where he was born.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Haldar Saheb:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">He was born in North India. Back then, the divisions of Aryavarta and Dakshinatya existed. His birthplace is known as Harikhetra, located in North India. Later, he moved to South India for his penance. Markandeya Muni's father was also from a lineage of sages.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Disciple:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">What was his father's name?</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Haldar Saheb:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">His father was a great rishi, but he was childless for a long time. He had no son or daughter. So, he performed penance to Lord Brahma, the creator. Brahma appeared before him and asked, "What do you desire? Do you want many sons, or just one?"</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Haldar Saheb:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">He explained that if he chose to have many sons, they would be powerful but unrighteous and oppressive—of a demonic nature. On the other hand, if he chose to have only one son, he would be exceptionally virtuous and wise, but he would have a very short life—only seven years.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Haldar Saheb:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">The Rishi, being a man of great character, thought, "I want a son who will bring glory to my lineage and whose fame will last for ages." He chose the virtuous son, even though his life would be short. Brahma granted the boon, and thus Markandeya was born.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Haldar Saheb:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">However, the parents only later realized the weight of the "short life" condition—that he would live for only seven years. This was the tragedy. As Markandeya approached his fifth year, his parents grew deeply anxious. His mother urged the father to seek Brahma's help again.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Haldar Saheb:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Brahma appeared but said, "I cannot change what has been decreed. My words must hold their value." The Rishi then turned to Lord Vishnu. Vishnu also said, "I cannot overturn Brahma's decree."</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Haldar Saheb:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Hearing his parents' sorrow, the five-year-old Markandeya said, "Mother, I will perform penance myself." With only two years of his life remaining, he headed south towards the sea. In the forest, he first met Rishi Pulastya, one of the Saptarishis. Pulastya asked, "Child, why are you performing such intense penance at this age?"</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Haldar Saheb:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Markandeya explained the situation. Pulastya advised him to go further south to the shore, where he would find Rishi Kundu, a great devotee of Shiva. Kundu Rishi welcomed him and gave him the Mahamrityunjaya Mantra.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Haldar Saheb:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Markandeya created a Shiva Lingam out of mud and began his worship. As the two years passed and he turned seven, the messengers of Yama (the God of Death) arrived with their ledgers to take his soul. However, Lord Shiva himself stood there to protect his devotee. There was a confrontation; the Puranas say Shiva's attendants even fought off Yama's messengers.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Haldar Saheb:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Yama himself then arrived. Shiva declared, "This boy is my devotee. No one else can be his master—not Brahma, not Vishnu, not you. I have granted him the Mahamrityunjaya blessing."</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Haldar Saheb:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Brahma and Vishnu also arrived and acknowledged that since Shiva had claimed him, the laws of mortal life no longer applied in the same way. Shiva granted him the boon to live through seven Kalpas.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Haldar Saheb:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">One Kalpa consists of the four Yugas—Satya, Treta, Dvapara, and Kali. Markandeya Muni is said to live through seven such cycles, meaning he remains alive even during the Pralaya (the great dissolution of the universe).</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Haldar Saheb:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">If you go to the Narmada River today, you will find the Markandeya Ashram. It is said that the river Reva (Narmada), who is considered the daughter of Shiva, also remains during the dissolution, and Markandeya remains with her.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Haldar Saheb:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">This story teaches us that through the worship of Shiva and the Mahamrityunjaya Mantra, one can overcome the fear of death and even the limitations of nature. Jagadguru Adi Shankaracharya also visited Omkareshwar, which is connected to these traditions.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Disciple:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">So, even though he was destined to live only seven years, he lived for seven Kalpas.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Haldar Saheb:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Exactly. That is the power of Mahamrityunjaya. We will discuss the Markandeya Purana in more detail another day with Gurudev.</span>
</div>

<div class="speaker-block" style="margin-bottom:2rem;border-left:3px solid var(--accent-color);padding-left:1.5rem;">
<span class="speaker-name" style="font-weight:700;color:var(--accent-bright);margin-right:0.5rem;text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:1px;font-size:1rem;">Disciple:</span>
<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">You have heard about Markandeya Muni from Gurudev and his disciples. Pranam, Gurudev.</span>
</div>

<span class="speech-text" style="display:block;margin-top:0.5rem;">Pranam, my son.</span>
