The King watched for a moment and thought, "What a cruel man! The cow is already injured, and he is whipping it." He summoned the man. When the man stood before the King, his appearance was repulsive. The King asked, "Who are you?" He replied, "Maharaj, I am Kali." The King said, "I have heard of you. You are destined to take over the earth. But why are you beating this cow? Can't you see it has only three legs?"

Kali replied, "Maharaj, please don't be offended. I was showing you what the oppression of Kali would look like. I am Kali. I will cut out people’s tongues, gouge out their eyes, and sell their kidneys. This is my nature—cruelty."

The King replied, "Your time will never come as long as I am alive."

Kali bowed to the King and left. Time passed, and the King eventually forgot about this encounter. One day, the King went out hunting (Mrigaya) with his soldiers. It was a common practice for Kings back then. Towards the evening, a massive storm broke out with heavy rain and wind. The soldiers were scattered; everyone fled in different directions. The King found himself alone and wondered what to do. 

This was actually Kali’s influence; he created the storm to give the King a sign of his arrival. The King climbed a tree with his bow and arrows and waited there. When morning came, he felt very thirsty. As he climbed down, Kali appeared to him again, laughing. 

The King asked, "Who are you?" He replied, "Maharaj, have you forgotten me? I am Kali. The time has come for me to enter your kingdom." Parikshit insisted, "No, that won't happen. I won't let you enter while I'm alive." Kali said, "We shall see. Time will tell."

The King tried to shoot arrows at him, but they all vanished. Kali laughed from different directions. Unable to defeat him, the King struck a deal. Kali said, "Maharaj, there's no need for conflict. Just give me four places to reside." The King asked, "Four places?" Kali said, "Yes, and it won't harm you." The King asked what those places were. 

Kali said, "I will stay where alcohol is consumed (liquor shops)." The King thought, "That’s a bad place anyway; it won't affect my subjects." Then Kali said, "I will stay where women are bought and sold (brothels)." The King thought, "Alcohol and prostitution are both vices; let him stay there." Then he asked for the other two. Kali said, "I will stay where gambling happens." The King agreed, thinking gambling is also a vice. Finally, Kali said, "Maharaj, I want one more place. I want to stay in gold."

The King was wearing a golden crown and golden ornaments. He didn't realize the trap. He said, "Fine, stay in gold." So, Kali established his influence in alcohol, prostitution, gambling, and gold. 

King Parikshit was still very thirsty. He saw a sage sitting under a tree, but the sage was in a deep trance (Nispricha), appearing almost lifeless. The King approached him and said, "I am the King of this land. I am thirsty. Please give me some water. I got lost in the storm." But the sage was in deep meditation; his soul was elsewhere, only his body remained. The King asked several times, but received no response. 

Because of the golden crown on the King's head, Kali was able to influence his mind. Kali whispered to him, "Look at this fake ascetic. He won't give you water. He’s just pretending." The King, feeling insulted, looked around and saw a dead snake. He picked it up with his bow and draped it around the sage's neck. He thought, "You didn't give me water, so here is your punishment." 

The King then left to find water. Meanwhile, the sage's son, Shringi, was returning from bathing in the Ganges. Seeing his father in that state, he went into a meditative state to find out who did it. He saw that King Parikshit was responsible. In anger, he took water from the Ganges and cursed him: "King Parikshit, you will die in seven days from a bite by the snake Takshaka. Otherwise, Kali will never be able to fully enter this world." 

It was all a divine plan. When the sage woke from his meditation, he scolded his son. He said, "Why did you do this? Parikshit is a great King who loves his subjects." The son replied, "I did it for the sake of truth. He insulted you by putting a dead snake around your neck, so I punished him." The sage realized the gravity of the situation. 

The sage went to the royal court to inform Parikshit. The King, recognizing the sage, was about to apologize, but the sage said, "My son has cursed you. You will die in seven days by Takshaka’s bite." The King was stunned by the fear of death and asked what he should do. The sage advised, "There is only one way. The King followed the advice, and on the seventh day, Takshaka bit and killed him. After Parikshit's death, Kali entered the world without any resistance. He stayed in those four places and gradually expanded his influence. What you see today is the result of that influence.
