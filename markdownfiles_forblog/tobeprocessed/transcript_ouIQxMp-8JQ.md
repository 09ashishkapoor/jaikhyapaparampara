Here is the clean, punctuated, and well-formatted transcript of the audio in English:

Interviewer: Pranam, Gurudev.

Gurudev: Pranam, pranam.

Interviewer: Gurudev, today is the 1st of Ashwin. On the 25th of Ashwin, which is the 13th of October, we have the annual festival of our temple. If you could please give a message regarding this annual festival to your beloved disciples and devotees...

Gurudev: Yes, every year we choose the night of Mahalaya. That Amavasya (New Moon) is a very auspicious and powerful one. Before that, there is also the Kousiki Amavasya, but we usually don’t perform these major rituals then. We work on the day of Mahalaya. On that day, we perform both Tara Puja and Kali Puja. There is Kali Puja, Tara Puja, and Homa-Yajna (sacrificial fire), all performed with great devotion.

The Kali idol is immersed after the puja, but Ma Tara remains with us always. Ma Tara’s Anga-rag (repainting/restoration) will be done in a day or two. They will come and decorate the Mother anew. This happens every year without fail. I have been witnessing this for the last 32 years. It always happens this way.

In the earlier days, there was no electricity, so we used to decorate with candles, and the puja was performed by candlelight. Later, after we got electricity, things became much more convenient. There is some music, song, and distribution of clothes and other items.

Like every year, we will celebrate this year as well. There is also an arrangement for food (Prasad) every year, and it will be there this year too. Those who visit regularly know about it. Coming early in the morning doesn't serve much of a purpose; the main preparations start in the evening. The puja doesn't begin before 10:00 PM.

According to our long-standing tradition, we first perform Mora Puja (ritual involving a corpse) and Chita Puja (ritual at the pyre). Only after these rituals does the Puja of the Mother begin. This has been our practice since the beginning. Every year on that night, a body arrives, and we perform the Mora Puja and Chita Puja before starting Ma’s Puja. This is our tradition, and I don’t think there will be any exception this year.

However, my health is quite poor lately, so I am unable to participate as much. Everyone tells me, "You don't have to sit for the rituals; we will manage." There is Susmanto and another person who comes from Midnapore; he performs the puja throughout the night. Another person I know performs the Kali Puja. Both pujas are performed with great devotion. It truly is a beautiful ceremony.

Previously, we used to offer fish and meat during the puja. But that stopped after we performed the Mahamritunjaya Yajna here for a thousand days. Since then, fish or any non-vegetarian items are no longer allowed inside. Our elders who performed the Mahamritunjaya said that fish and meat should not be brought in anymore. So, we stopped. Ma used to be offered fish and rice earlier; she used to eat fish. But since we performed that Yajna for three years, those things are gone. I still have photos of the Mother being worshipped with fish and meat. We never ate the fish ourselves; it was offered to the sacrificial fire (Homa). Both fish and meat were cut into pieces and offered to the fire. No one was allowed to eat it. 28 pieces of meat were offered to the fire. That's how the Homa was performed.

The puja used to go on all night. In the past, we couldn't even finish it on time. Now, things have changed a bit, and people do what they can. They don't even let me stay late because I fell ill two years in a row during the night rituals. They tell me to leave by 11:00 PM or 12:00 AM and say they will handle the rest. This is how it’s been lately. I’ve become quite frail, and I feel bad about it. I used to stay awake all night. There would be Kirtan and singing at dawn; it felt wonderful. It still happens now—the singing and the puja are done beautifully. The priests who come are very good and perform the rituals with great care.

This is a special occasion for us, and we want the puja to be done well. I don’t care about the grandeur, but the puja must be done correctly. Everything is arranged. Since the Mahamritunjaya Yajna and the establishment of the Shiva temple, non-vegetarian offerings have been completely removed.

Interviewer: Yes, please continue.

Gurudev: On the 13th of October, the 25th of Ashwin, which is a Friday, it is the day of the annual puja. It’s an Amavasya, and there is also a Solar Eclipse on that day. This is a bit concerning because a Solar Eclipse usually doesn't fall on that day; we’ve never seen it before. Usually, we finish the puja, and as we finish the immersion of the Mother and return, we sit down to listen to the Mahalaya recitations. This happens every year. We finish the puja, immerse the idol, and then listen to Mahalaya.

Interviewer: Gurudev, for this annual festival and the feast for the devotees, a lot of people come to eat. There must be a lot of expenses. Where does this money come from?

Gurudev: Well, to be honest, earlier we had nothing. Now we have some funds. Everyone contributes. All the disciples and devotees who come and stay the night contribute something. How else could we manage? The expenses are nearly one lakh rupees. There are costs for the music, cloth distribution, and the feast. The expenses are high, but it gets covered. Our disciples who love and respect us come and give what they can. I don't even have to ask.

Interviewer: So, it can be said that whatever they feel like giving to the temple, they bring it.

Gurudev: Yes, once they know about it, they come. They come from far-off places like Assam, Cooch Behar, and Jharkhand. Everyone gives something. I don't even have a bank account; whatever comes in is used for the temple.

Interviewer: And you don't take any money personally.

Gurudev: I don't take money because I see how hard people work to earn it. They earn it with their blood and sweat. How can I take that? To me, taking that money is like consuming poison. I see hundreds of people, and even if there is no food in my house, I won't take a single rupee for myself. No one can say Gurudev asked for a single rupee. I have done so much work for people, big tasks, but I never took a penny. Taking people's hard-earned money is like swallowing a cobra's venom. That is my personal opinion. I have always said that taking money from people for personal gain and then feasting on it is something I strongly dislike.

Interviewer: Gurudev, please invite all your disciples and devotees for the 25th of Ashwin, Friday, for the annual festival.

Gurudev: Yes, I invite everyone to come and participate in our annual puja.

Interviewer: Gurudev, Hari Om. We will end it here today.

Gurudev: Wait, on what date?

Interviewer: 25th of Ashwin.

Gurudev: 25th of Ashwin, Friday, 13th of October. Our annual Mahalaya festival will take place. It starts the night before Mahalaya. I request everyone who visits us or looks after this temple to come. If everyone contributes a little, there are no problems with the expenses. I invite everyone. We also have some guests, about 20-25 people like the local police, MPs, and MLAs. They are invited and, surprisingly, they all come. We used to have big meetings with songs and dances; that has decreased a bit, but the invitations are still sent to everyone, from MPs to MLAs, and they all attend.

Interviewer: Gurudev, we will end here today. Pranam.

Gurudev: Pranam, pranam.
