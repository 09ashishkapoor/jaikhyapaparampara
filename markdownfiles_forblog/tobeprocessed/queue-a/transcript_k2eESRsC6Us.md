What happens to people after death? || by Guptasadhak Shyamakyapa YouTube Link: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2eESRsC6Us

This is a transcript of the audio, translated into English for clarity and well-formatted for readability.

(Sound of bells and a conch shell)

Host: Pranam, Baba.

Guru: Pranam.

Host: Gurudev, after a long time, I wanted to record an episode with you today.

Guru: Yes, go ahead.

Host: Gurudev, so many people die. When they go, they don't take clothes, food, or money. They leave this world naked. Where do they go? What do they eat? How do they survive?

Guru: You have raised a very profound question. It is a major subject for contemplation. Science will not suffice here; you must follow the Shastras (scriptures). Without the scriptures, you cannot reconcile these mysteries. Science alone cannot explain them. A person dies and leaves everything behind—they are indeed naked. What you said is correct and very interesting.

Where do these souls go, and what do they eat? There is a saying: "One is born in a mother's home after ten months and ten days; if you do not recognize the path then, you will find a noose around your neck." We must consider that period of ten months and ten days. Within this time, most souls find a womb.

Now, what do our scriptures say? They say something quite remarkable. When the Pinda (ritual food offering for the deceased) is offered, it is believed that the nourishment from that offering sustains the soul for an entire year. They do not need to eat anything else; their hunger remains satisfied. It is an amazing concept—that a single offering keeps them full for a year.

Regarding that gap of ten months and ten days, many souls wait. Some find a womb quickly, while others do not. In their own consciousness, they might think, "No, I want to return to my own family." They can wait for 10 or 15 years. They won’t enter just any womb; they wait for one within their own kin. Whether you look at it scientifically, tantrically, or scripturally, it is a fascinating phenomenon.

After that waiting period, they enter a womb. Then they are born, wear new clothes... even physical needs like hunger and waste are managed differently initially because of the sustenance from the Pinda.

The Puranas mention that the soul must cross nineteen kingdoms to reach the gates of Yamraj (the King of Death). Yama judges them there. He decides whether the soul goes to heaven, hell, or which of the 8.4 million types of wombs it will enter next.

Host: According to one’s Karma (deeds).

Guru: Yes. It is said: "Whatever you did while laughing here, Chitragupta recorded in his ledger. Your judge will be the Creator, before whom no trickery works." Bribes and money do not work there. There is a fair judgment, which determines your next destination.

However, some souls are adamant about returning only to their own relatives. You may have noticed people saying a child "looks exactly like his father" or behaves like him. Some souls wait for years until a suitable womb is available within their own family line. Until then, they remain in a subtle body (Sookshma Deha), wandering, but not taking birth.

I was reading about this and wondering if it’s possible. It is said that the Pinda offered during the funeral rites sustains the soul for a year.

Host: But how do they survive for 10 or 15 years if they wait that long?

Guru: That is a valid point. They are waiting. For instance, if a spouse passes away and the survivor chooses to remain faithful, the departed soul might wait a long time for a reunion of souls. It is an extraordinary thing. This cycle of coming and going...

Host: It’s like a process of recycling.

Guru: It is. And it only stops when one attains Moksha (liberation). Dharma, Artha, Kama, Moksha. If one attains Moksha, they are not reborn into this world. They ascend to the heavenly realms and dwell among the deities—perhaps in Vaikuntha or with Shiva in the Himalayas. They stay there, serving the gods.

Host: But Gurudev, if they eat the Pinda meant for one year, but do not find a womb in ten months—say they wait for 10 years to be born to their own daughter or son—how do they survive those remaining years? What do they eat?

Guru: By then, they have already made their choice.

Host: No, I mean if they haven't entered a womb yet during those years.

Guru: It is said they take refuge with a deity based on their past deeds. One must eventually return; this cycle is mandatory. "He who is born must die; where is immortality?" Many have said this in the Puranas.

I often ponder this—the food provided for ten months and ten days through the Pinda. One must take birth eventually. Perhaps 1% or 2% attain Moksha and do not return. They only descend when the gods instruct them to do so for a noble purpose. Then they enter a womb and inherit the qualities of a great soul.

This process has been ongoing since time immemorial. Imagine, a person goes without money or clothes for months... how do they exist?

Host: It is a lot to think about; it could even give one a headache.

Guru: It is profound. It is difficult to fully grasp that state. I have studied much, performed funeral rites, and I know the mantras and their meanings. But ultimately, it is a matter of faith.

Host: Is there a connection between souls and nature?

Guru: It is said that the body is composed of the Pancha Bhoota (five elements): Kshiti (earth), Ap (water), Tej (fire/energy), Marut (air), and Vyom (space/ether). This Vyom is the gaseous substance within. We chant "Bam Bam" for Shiva; "Bam" refers to this gaseous element. You cannot see it, but it exists within the womb. These five elements form your body. All of this is present in the womb—earth, water, air. When a human takes form and is born, they cannot speak initially. But as they grow...

Host: Forgive me, but isn't it said that God makes them mute so they forget their past lives? Because they supposedly remember everything at first.

Guru: Science says that upon birth, a baby is subjected to temperature changes—cold and hot water—and is made to cry. That cry, the "Wha" sound, is said to make them forget their previous existence. They call out for "Ma" (mother). At that time, they have no language other than that cry. These things make me wonder: which path should we follow—science or the scriptures?

Host: Science was, is, and will be. The scriptures were there before and will always be.

Guru: Even today, people follow the scriptures. When a person dies, science often yields to ritual and scripture; it admits defeat. Even after death, scriptural traditions are maintained. It is a marvel.

Host: Gurudev, many people have questions and many thoughts on this.

Guru: You should all reflect on it. You are a living being and will die one day. Having some understanding of death and what follows can be helpful. Remember, you will depart this world naked.

Host: Every human should understand this to grasp the concept of sin and virtue.

Guru: Exactly. You go naked and take no money. If you are thirsty, there is water on earth. And for food, you have the sustenance from the Pinda. These thoughts often occupy my mind. I, too, will die one day. I think about what truly constitutes a human—science or the scriptures? People live by science, yet after death, the scriptures become paramount.

Host: Science is external; the essence is...

Guru: Science only takes over at the moment of birth. Until then, the scriptures are at work. I contemplate these things deeply, though I don't often share them.

Host: Gurudev, we have learned so much today. The viewers and listeners have too.

Guru: It is important to know because death is inevitable. Scientists also suggest there is an existence after death. An American scientist spoke of this—that death isn't the end. However, one cannot truly know without experiencing it.

I had a very dear friend. We were inseparable. He once told me, "Shyamal, when I die, I will tell you everything about what happens on the other side. Don't worry." But he never returned.

Host: Gurudev, the episode has become quite long.

Guru: These subjects are naturally extensive.

Host: We have learned a lot. Stay well, Gurudev. I pray for everyone’s well-being. We will end it here.

Guru: Truly, the environment has become troubled. Doctors perform so many tests today, yet often find nothing. Recently, some people came to me saying they had visited Vellore for treatment but still don't understand their ailment.

Host: Pollution is causing mysterious diseases that baffle even the best doctors.

Guru: A bank manager came to me with persistent liver pain. He had tried everything. Finally, he sought my help. I told him we would see what could be done.

Host: Pranam, Gurudev. We will leave it here for today.
