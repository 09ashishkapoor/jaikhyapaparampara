Interviewer: Pranam, Gurudev.

Gurudev: Pranam, pranam. Yes, speak.

Interviewer: Tomorrow is India’s 74th Independence Day. Many martyrs sacrificed their lives for this independence, but many of their dreams remain unfulfilled. Please inspire and awaken the current generation so they can work toward fulfilling those dreams.

Gurudev: Look, independence did happen, but it happened away from the public eye, at midnight. Many had advised Jawaharlal Nehru and Gandhi not to declare independence on that day. However, it was later seen that after discussing it among themselves, they took independence at 12 o’clock at night, like thieves—yes, I call them thieves.

The general public didn’t even realize they had become independent; they were fast asleep. They never truly knew when India became free. Because of this lack of awareness, even today, no one truly feels that India is independent. I don’t see any signs of real independence.

The souls of the brave martyrs who sacrificed themselves are not at peace. Their souls or the "Supreme Soul" are not at rest. They didn't get what they truly wanted. During the Congress government’s time, there was no end to the fraud, cheating, and theft. This is what we gained from independence—thieves and cheaters. A pack of jackals gathered back then, and it was they who "stole" India’s independence. As a result, even at 74 years of age, we feel we haven't gained true freedom. Whether we ever will is doubtful. This is not how independence should be.

Today, no one remembers the great men who gave their lives—Kanailal, Prafulla Chaki, Khudiram Bose, Master Da [Surya Sen], Bagha Jatin. Who remembers them? Even Netaji [Subhash Chandra Bose] was once branded a traitor. These leaders never wanted this kind of independence for India.

Even today, there are many people who do not want India to be truly free. I am not pointing fingers at Hindus or Muslims specifically; I am not mentioning any particular caste. But it is tragic. We shout about being independent, we tell stories, and we put on shows. But are we really free? We are still living under a reign of subjection. Our voices are throttled. We aren't allowed to write; we aren't allowed to speak. If you write or say something, the police show up at your house. What is the value of this kind of independence?

Think about Netaji. He tried so hard for India’s freedom, and what did they call him? A traitor? Is there anything worse than that? If we want to taste true independence today, the document—the Constitution—must be changed. Many things need to be overhauled. Otherwise, real independence will not come to India.

The same group of people continues to enjoy the benefits of this "independence," and their stance is that India doesn't need real freedom. They only pray to God for more opportunities to loot. Everything is being ruined. A few groups like "Dal" and "Pathan" are causing chaos. There is no real independence; don't even call it that. It is called a "Red Letter Day," a special day of remembrance, but we feel this remembrance has no value.

You shout about independence, but for what? Today, if you say one word, the police arrive. If you write something, the police arrive, or you face death. This independence is unnecessary. India was better off under subjection. The British ruled us, and now these people are ruling us. It’s the same footsteps; there is no change.

Interviewer: Do you have any hope for the future?

Gurudev: Look, the government is currently run by the BJP. In the state, we have Mamata Banerjee, and at the center, we have Modi Ji. I don't know what their policies are. Regarding COVID-19—this coronavirus—they knew about it long before. Why did they hide it? Why wasn't protection taken earlier? Even today, there is no real protection. Does a two-day lockdown make the virus leave the country?

What is needed is a social awakening. People need to interact with each other properly, maintain distance, and wash their hands and feet well. These are essential. But how many people are doing this? If you go to the market, you see people on top of each other as if there’s a famine. They think, "I won't get food, let me grab everything and fill the fridge." They think only of themselves and ignore everyone else. There is plenty of food in the market, yet one class of people thinks they won't get any more, so they loot and eat. They don't care if they die of corona. This has become the mindset of people.

How can a society without consciousness or conscience grow? We must awaken our conscience. Otherwise, nothing will change. People still have that old mentality: "I will eat, and everyone else can go to hell." They are looting and eating, yet today they act like saints. As Rabindranath Tagore said, "You, Maharaja, became a saint today, and I became the thief." That is the reality.

Interviewer: So, based on what you’re saying, the dreams of those who gave their lives for independence are largely unfulfilled.

Gurudev: Their souls have no peace. None at all. We are suffering the consequences. A few "jackals and dogs" have gathered. They never wanted India’s independence, and I say this loudly today: they never wanted it. So jumping around and shouting about independence is useless. Those who truly loved and wanted independence didn't get it, and those who didn't want it are enjoying its fruits.

As Tagore said: "In this world, alas, he who has the most wants even more; the King's hand steals the wealth of the poor." These people are stealing from the poor. They used to beg for food, and now they are rich by stealing from the destitute. What is the value in that?

Interviewer: Gurudev, will any spiritual great man arrive in the future to change this?

Gurudev: He will surely come. Look, if Krishna could be born during Kansa’s tyranny, then amidst this oppression, someone will be born—or perhaps has already been born—whom we do not yet know.

A saffron-clad, pocket-less saint will come to rule India. He will establish "Hindutva." It’s already happening bit by bit. Kashmir happened. The Ram Mandir happened. Triple Talaq was abolished. Now, only the "Swiss Bank" issue remains to be broken. Once that is done, things will be mostly settled.

But remember, the King might write on a tree: "Do not kill the deer, it is forbidden." But the deer will still be killed, and humans will kill it. No matter how much we say "Eliminate Poverty"—as Sonia Gandhi and Indira Gandhi once did—poverty wasn't eliminated. Instead, they were eliminated. Those who gave the slogan are now gone. These are false promises and false words used to secure their own positions.

We tell people two things: either become a saint so you can eat, or join politics so you can eat. Either call upon God and become a saint to get a meal, or enter politics. That’s what we say.

Interviewer: Gurudev, given the current situation, do you hope the youth of the next generation will rise up?

Gurudev: The youth will not rise yet. It is difficult to raise this youth. Who raises the youth? Only those who are honest and true. Who is honest here?

Interviewer: But then India will go to ruin, Gurudev.

Gurudev: It is already going down. It is constantly sinking. There is nothing we can do unless a great man, a Mahapurush, takes charge. Just as Krishna dealt with Kansa, someone must come for the current state of society. If no one comes, then the Gita is a lie.

"Yada yada hi dharmasya glanir bhavati bharata / abhyutthanam adharmasya tadatmanam srjamy aham / paritranaya sadhunam vinasaya ca duskrtam / dharma-samsthapanarthaya sambhavami yuge yuge"

(Whenever there is a decline in righteousness and a rise in unrighteousness, O Bharata, I manifest Myself. To protect the righteous, to destroy the wicked, and to re-establish the principles of dharma, I appear age after age.)

If no one comes, I will believe the Gita and the words of Krishna’s mouth are false. But someone will come. We are waiting. we are calling upon God. Someone will come. I can already hear the footsteps. He will come to deliver the saints and punish the wicked, the frauds, and the cheaters. This cannot be a lie. The Gita is truth; the world is an illusion. It cannot be false. So, there’s no point in overthinking it. It’s a matter of time. When the time comes, everything will be alright. We are waiting for that pocket-less, saffron-clad saint to rule India and establish Hindutva. This country was divided on the basis of religion—Hindustan and Pakistan. Yet, we cannot even loudly say we are "Hindustani." We cannot say we are Hindu. Our voices are suppressed, while they put on a show of independence and raise flags. What is the value of such independence? To me, it has no value. Don’t provoke me further, or I might say something even more unpleasant.

Namaskar.

Interviewer: Pranam, Gurudev.

Gurudev: Pranam, pranam.
