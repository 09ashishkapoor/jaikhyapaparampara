Speaker 1 (Host): Namaskar, Gurudev.

Gurudev: Namaskar, Namaskar.

Host: First, I would like to tell our friends to subscribe to our channel, guptasadhakshyamakhapa.com. The more you subscribe, the more valuable videos we will be able to bring to you. Today, we have with us Mr. Sujit Haldar. He is a writer. Gurudev, would you like to say something about Mr. Haldar?

Gurudev: Sujit Haldar has been coming to me for a long time. He is a prominent writer; you will find many of his books in the market. Today, he wishes to ask me some questions that have been on his mind. Yes, tell me, what would you like to know?

Sujit Haldar: Gurudev, please accept my respects. It’s not exactly a question—I don’t have the audacity to question you—but rather some inquiries that have been bothering me. Since I started coming here, I’ve heard you say quite often that we live in a "Neimoy Jagat" (a world of non-existence or void). It would be very helpful for us, the listeners and viewers, if you could explain what this "Neimoy Jagat" really is.

Gurudev: You are right. I often tell people that we live in a "Neimoy Jagat." What is this world? In this world, nothing is yours. You have no contribution here. Everything is the contribution of the Mother (the Divine). You are forcefully claiming, "I am doing this, I am doing that," but in reality, we live in a world of void. There is the sky above; you get to eat, you get to sleep—these are all gifts from God. You are at peace; that too is God's gift. We live in a world where nothing truly exists for us. We might live for 60 or 70 years and think we are achieving so much, but can you take anything with you? You might use things for a few days, then you discard them or put them away. Humans cannot use any one thing forever; they eventually get bored. Something might seem great at first, but after a few days, you lose interest and look for something else. That’s why I call it "Neimoy Jagat." The only eternal essence is God. We say the world is an illusion and God is the truth.

Sujit Haldar: So, Gurudev, are you speaking from the perspective of Jagatguru Adi Shankaracharya’s philosophy—Brahma Satyam Jagan Mithya (Brahman is the truth, the world is an illusion)?

Gurudev: Exactly. Many may not know the meaning of Brahmattwa (God-realization). Perhaps one in a hundred million attains it. It is the most difficult path. It requires immense time, perseverance, and many spiritual practices to reach that realization. When someone attains that state, they themselves might not even realize it initially. It is called the Turiya state. That is when titles like "Paramahansa" are given, like Ramakrishna Paramahansa or Tailanga Swami. It means being completely immersed within the Divine.

Sujit Haldar: Gurudev, we can't all reach that Turiya level. But regarding this "world of void," doesn’t that make our human existence feel precarious?

Gurudev: Not at all. You have your own identity, and you must work with it. As the Gita says, Karmanyevadhikaraste Ma Phaleshu Kadachana—you have the right to work, but not to the fruits of your labor. You must perform your duties. Since you have taken birth, you must act. But if you don't obsess over the results, doesn't it become a "Neimoy Jagat"? You do so much work, but you don't claim the result. This is what Krishna taught.

Sujit Haldar: That brings to mind something else you mentioned—that from the moment of birth, the Panchabhuta (five elements) start consuming us.

Gurudev: Yes, every day you are entering deeper into that void. When we are in the womb, our body is formed by the Panchabhuta: Kshiti (Earth), Ap (Water), Tej (Energy/Fire), Marut (Air), and Vyom (Space/Ether). Kshiti means the earth or soil that forms our flesh. Ap is water; we are surrounded by fluids in the womb. Marut is air; without it, you cannot survive. Tej is heat; we survive on the mother's body heat. Vyom refers to the gaseous substances and trillions of bacteria that help you breathe. If these bacteria die, your breath stops and you die. So, even our breathing is dependent on others. This is a high level of science that many don't realize—that we are supported by 65 trillion bacteria.

Sujit Haldar: So, Vyom, which we thought was just "emptiness," is actually quite active?

Gurudev: It is empty in one sense, but in the womb, there is fluid, air, and the mother's energy. When you are born onto this earth, the five elements slowly start reclaiming you. As the saint Ramprasad sang: "Nothing stays in your hands; whatever you earn by hard work is consumed by the five elements." They are slowly "eating" you away every day, and one day, you will be completely reclaimed by them. That is what death is—merging back into the Panchabhuta.

Sujit Haldar: That is a new perspective. We usually think we merge with the elements only after death, but you're saying it starts from birth.

Gurudev: It starts from the moment of conception. They create you, and then they slowly consume you until you merge back. This is why nothing is truly ours. We are like tenants here. We stay for 50, 70, or 80 years, pay our "rent" through our actions, and then we have to leave. Apart from our waste, we don't really give back much; we are always asking the Mother for more.

Sujit Haldar: And then there are the Shada Ripu (six internal enemies/vices).

Gurudev: Yes—lust, anger, greed, attachment, pride, and envy. These six "crocodiles" are always chasing you, driving you to do this and that. Ramprasad said that if you "smear the turmeric of wisdom on your body," these crocodiles won't touch you. They are always trying to tempt you. You might say you won't fall for it, but you do. However, if you always remember that you live in a "Neimoy Jagat," you can find some relief. Observe the world, but don't be consumed by it. Don't just pluck the flower; admire it. The world is a Kuhak (a mirage or illusion). As the Chandi says, even after hearing all this, people fall into the same trap because of the play of the six vices.

Sujit Haldar: Gurudev, we have been very enriched by this conversation. Please accept my respects.

Gurudev: Blessings to you.
