Transcript: Experiences of the Narmada Parikrama

Host: Pronam (Respectful greetings), Gurudev.

Gurudev: Yes, Pronam, brother.

Host: I offer my respects to our viewers and begin today’s new episode. Today’s episode is unique; you will learn a lot of information. Sitting right next to Gurudev is his disciple, Chandan Adhikari. He has recently returned from the Narmada Parikrama (circumambulation of the Narmada River). I won’t say much more; you should hear the rest from him and Gurudev.

Gurudev: Yes, you may have seen another boy from our Shantikunj, nicknamed "Pagla Baba," who traveled to Kamakhya on foot. Everyone knows that. After him, Chandan Adhikari went for the Narmada Parikrama on behalf of Shantikunj. Before leaving, he told me, "Gurudev, I am going to see Mother Narmada." I said, "Fine, go." I gave him some rules and rituals that we used to follow long ago. He followed them... though things have changed much since then. Suddenly, he felt a transformation and decided to do the full Parikrama. Now, a Parikrama is no small feat—it traditionally takes three years, three months, and thirteen days. Within that period, there are "Chaturmas" (four-month holy breaks). Right now, it is Chaturmas, so everything is closed. It will reopen after Kali Puja.

Chandan: People are also traveling by road nowadays. Using vehicles is faster. But there is another way—on foot.

Gurudev: On foot, but you must walk along the banks of the Mother (the river). Walking along the banks is the true Parikrama. It’s just you and the Mother; you walk together, chanting her name or your Guru-mantra. Even in this deep Kali Yuga, you can feel that the Mother is truly present there. You won't realize it otherwise. Your thoughts and resolutions manifest quickly; if you think of something, you might find it granted within two kilometers. It is a strange and miraculous place. Moreover, many great sages live along the banks of the Narmada. There are many ashrams where they perform penance. And the most important thing there is Seva (service). We often talk about religion as just visiting temples, but there, religion is service.

Chandan: The service there is incredible. You can’t imagine it. They take care of everything—your tea, your snacks, your meals. Even if you are a stranger, they treat you as their own. You cannot even bring "punya" (spiritual merit) home because they make you spend it all there through their kindness. There is no discrimination between rich and poor. Another amazing thing is the honesty. If you leave your belongings at a ghat and walk a kilometer away, your things will remain exactly as they were. No one will even look at them with greed.

Host: Very trustworthy people.

Chandan: Completely trustworthy. The tribal people there are poor, but their hearts are full of devotion. They say "Devotion is God." And truly, Mother has provided for them. They have no greed for money; they are satisfied with whatever little they earn. Sometimes the path goes through dense jungles. The most miraculous thing is that the wild animals there won't even look at you. There are tigers and leopards, but in the history of the Parikrama, no pilgrim—whether a monk or a householder—has ever been harmed by an animal. It is said to be a blessing from Lord Shiva. Even if a tiger comes in front of you, it just turns away. All you have to do is say "Har Har Narmade." Once, we encountered highly venomous snakes. We stopped in fear. The monks behind us asked, "Did you say 'Narmade Har'?" I said no. They said, "That’s what you must say." As soon as I chanted "Narmade Har," the snakes lowered their heads and went their way. You might feel fear initially, but after walking a few kilometers, the fear vanishes. That area is the meditation ground for 8.4 million (Chaurasi Lakh) sages. Many people don't even know that the lineages (Gotras) we name during our prayers belong to these sages who are still there. It’s a hidden, sacred place.

Chandan: Currently, many people from Maharashtra do the Parikrama. It is said that 33 crore (330 million) deities have meditated there. Every ghat has a name and a significance. As you go deeper into the forest, you find hidden sages who have been meditating for years. If you are lucky, they might call you for "Seva." They offer tea or rotis made of millet (jowar/maize) from their sacred fires (dhuni). They don't say much, and you shouldn't ask questions. Their main motto is "Sach Bolo" (Speak the Truth). The Narmada is roughly...

Gurudev: 3,320 kilometers long.

Chandan: Yes, and it has increased because of dams like the Sardar Sarovar Dam. There is a legend that Bhima (from the Mahabharata) proposed marriage to Narmada. When she refused, he tried to block her path. She escaped, and he cursed her, saying that in the Kali Yuga, she would be blocked at every step and her water would be distributed to every village. Now, that prophecy is coming true through these dams. I also heard from sages there that a time will come when all the rivers in India will dry up, and only Mother Narmada’s water will keep humanity alive because she is immortal.

Chandan: The sages there have no fear because they live in a state of truth and service. They don't predict the future; they just focus on their own spiritual practice. They might guide you if you make a mistake, but otherwise, they leave you to your own devotion.

Host: Did you walk all day and stay somewhere at night?

Chandan: We would walk about 10–15 kilometers, starting at 4 AM until about 10 AM before the sun got too hot. The water of the Narmada is miraculous. In the winter evenings, the water feels warm, and in the scorching heat of the afternoon, the water is ice-cold. It is perfectly fit for drinking. The natural beauty is indescribable. They say 900 rivers join the Narmada. Mother Narmada takes responsibility for you the moment you start. If you are in danger, she appears in some form to help. If someone doesn't believe this, they should do the Parikrama with a clean heart.

Chandan: There are monks from the Ramakrishna Mission, Bharat Sevashram Sangha, and other great orders meditating there. Some remain silent (Moun Vrat) all day. Some eat nothing but tea and water. During festivals like Ram Navami, massive feasts (Bhandara) are organized. You’ll wonder where the food comes from in the middle of a remote forest, but the villagers bring everything on tractors. The government is also providing more facilities now because more women and elderly couples are undertaking the journey.

Chandan: Mother Narmada is also called "Reva" because she was born from Shiva on a Sunday. She is extremely responsive to prayers. Sometimes she appears as a young girl to help you. Once, I was high on a mountain, exhausted and thirsty with no water source nearby. Suddenly, a tribal girl appeared and said, "Babaji, please drink some water." She said she was there to collect wood. After she left, other pilgrims who arrived said they hadn't seen anyone. That is her "Chhalana" (divine play). They also call her Krishna's sister because of her playful nature. It is said that the seven immortals (Chiranjeevis) like Ashwatthama, Hanuman, Vibhishana, Parshurama, and Rishi Markandeya still reside there in various disguises—sometimes as beggars or even foul-smelling outcasts to test your devotion.

Chandan: You don't need much money. The locals will help you with shoes or clothes if yours are torn. They consider it a blessing to serve pilgrims. I wish all of India was like the banks of the Narmada—free from envy and hatred.

Host: That’s a beautiful point. You mentioned that you must leave your "punya" (merit) there.

Chandan: Yes, because the devotion there is so pure. In other places, we might push a monk off a train or bus over money. But there, people will beg you to take their help. Even ticket collectors (TTEs) won't ask for a ticket if they see you are a "Parikramavasi" (pilgrim).

Host: Is there a specific dress code?

Chandan: Yes, usually an unstitched white dhoti. Sages wear what their Gurus prescribe, but for common people, it's a white dhoti and kurta. You follow the Sanatan Dharma and the path of the Vedas.

Host: The very sages who started our lineages (Gotras) are there.

Chandan: Yes, sages like Shandilya, Vishwamitra, and many others have their caves there. There are places associated with the Pandavas, and the ashram where Valmiki raised Luv and Kush. We are very lucky that someone from our Shantikunj completed this.

Chandan: Gurudev, it is my good fortune that I could do this under your guidance. I didn't even know Mother Narmada's name before I met you.

Gurudev: I was surprised. Chandan had a job, but he left it. I asked him, "Why leave your job in this economy?" He said, "I must go to Narmada." He came to me for advice and blessings the day before he left. It's wonderful to hear these experiences from a disciple's mouth.

Chandan: My only request to anyone going there is: do not litter or be dishonest. If you are insincere, the Mother will drive you away. But if you have devotion, she will call you. I met a German lady there who has been doing "Go Seva" (serving cows) for 20–25 years. She speaks better Hindi than we do!

Host: Alright, viewers, we will end here today. Stay healthy and well. Pronam, Gurudev.

Gurudev: Pronam, son.
