Self-knowledge (Who am I?) | What did the mystic Shyamakhapa say?

This transcript captures the conversation between **Anirban** (interviewer) and **Gurudev** (spiritual leader). The background features consistent clanking sounds, likely from a workshop or metalworking area.

***

**Anirban:** Salutations, Gurudev.

**Gurudev:** Salutations.

**Anirban:** Greetings to our viewers. Let’s begin today’s episode. Gurudev, there is something I am very curious to know. We saw an elderly woman come to you; she seemed quite aged. She told you, “Father, you have given me a second birth.” She meant that she was previously blind and couldn’t see, but through your blessings, her sight was restored. She was crying at your feet yesterday, saying she had found a new life. Gurudev, is this really possible? How did it happen? Please tell us.

**Gurudev:** Look, Anirban, there is a saying: “The storm kills the crows, and the Fakir’s (holy man’s) miracle grows.” Do you understand?

**Anirban:** No, Gurudev.

**Gurudev:** When a storm comes, many crows die. Then a Fakir claims, “See my power? Look at how many crows I’ve killed!” Do you get it? That’s why they say the storm does the work, but the holy man gets the credit. In my case, it’s the same. I don’t know who healed her or how it happened, but the credit was placed on me—that I was the one who did it. Even that lady said so. I didn’t really do anything. I just touched her eyes; it was a natural gesture. People often come to me complaining of back pain, knee pain, or vision loss.

**Anirban:** But Gurudev, I must interrupt—this happened through your blessing, didn't it? She said she hadn’t been able to see for a long time.

**Gurudev:** Like I said: the storm kills the crows, and the Fakir’s miracle grows. I am just a Fakir; my "miracle" was seen. But that’s not the whole story. Perhaps there was a blockage somewhere. When I touched her, I told her, “Go and see a different doctor.” She trusted me, saw another doctor, and that doctor said, “Yes, you will be fine, we just need to operate.” They operated, and she got her sight back. So, where is my "miracle" in that?

**Anirban:** But she had been seeing doctors for years and years, taking medicine, and nothing worked. Then she came to you, you touched her eyes, and you advised her to change her doctor. She took your blessing, had the surgery, and now she can see. She said, “I couldn’t see before, but now I can see my Guru.”

**Gurudev:** Again, I say: the storm kills the crows, the miracle grows. People think it happened because of me. Yes, God has given us a certain power. Based on that power, I told her to see another doctor. She eventually saw a specialist who confirmed she would be fine. When she was still blind, she asked me for *Diksha* (initiation). I told her, “I won’t give you *Diksha* until your eyes are healed. How can I initiate you if you can't even see?” Anyway, now she says she can see and wants the initiation.

**Anirban:** Gurudev, it’s not just about blindness. Recently, a man came whose kidneys had failed. His creatinine level was around 6.9 or 7. But with your blessing, he is doing well—no dialysis, nothing. He is perfectly fine, just by your blessing. That is also possible, Gurudev.

**Gurudev:** It’s better not to talk about these things. It increases ego and vanity. However, there is something to be said about the power of "touch." I won’t deny that. I have practiced *Sadhana* (spiritual discipline) for many years; Mother (the Divine) must have given me something. Sometimes, a touch makes people feel better. There have been many such cases—people's eyes, various ailments...

**Anirban:** And cancer. I have seen many cancer patients coming to you.

**Gurudev:** Regarding that... these are cases where people usually don’t survive. Some might linger on. Once, a doctor told me, “Just keep this person alive a bit longer so he can see his son’s wedding.” The wedding happened, everything went well, and he passed away a year later. 

There is no "answer" to cancer. Yet, sometimes it can be cured. My own wife had cancer twelve years ago. It was the final stage. She is still alive and healthy today. You’ve seen Guruma; she is doing well. Yes, we spent three or four lakh rupees on treatment, but she survived. We can't always explain why these things happen. It feels good when people come and tell us they are better. If a touch helps them, I won't deny it. I take that responsibility. But as I said, it’s the storm and the miracle. 

**Anirban:** Gurudev, let’s move to another topic. Savitri Devi from Mumbai has written to you. She is asking about *Atmagyan*—Self-knowledge. She wants a video on “Who am I?” How can one know oneself through *Sadhana*? Is it something you realize yourself, or does someone else certify it? If you need a certificate from others to know yourself, then what is the value of that knowledge? She says she gets confused and frustrated, feeling she won't attain this knowledge in this lifetime and that her life is wasted. She says that even with a Guru like you, if she can't understand, what is the point? She wants you to shed some light on this.

**Gurudev:** She is asking: Who am I? It’s a clear question. There are three parts to this: Who am I? Where do I come from? And what is my duty—what is my work here? These three together comprise *Atmagyan*.

We speak of *Omkar*. We say *Soham*—"I am That." I can say with conviction: "I am that person whom you can trust." If you trust, you will find peace. You will get whatever you seek. This is *Soham*. Who am I? I am *Soham*. Remember me, have faith in me, and you will receive what you desire. This is the essence of Self-knowledge.

However, not everyone attains this. It’s a matter of God’s grace. When a person truly recognizes themselves, they move into a different realm. Honestly, you all come here, we sit and talk, but we live in a different world. This ordinary world is not ours.

We have attained *Atmagyan*. There is a concept called *Omkar*—a vibration through which great things were accomplished in the past. 

**Anirban:** Like the sound "Om"?

**Gurudev:** Actually, "Om" is a simplified version. The real sound is "A-U-M" (Aum). It is very powerful. There is a chant: *Om Am Arkamandalaya Dwadasha Kalatmane Namah*. If you want to be like the Sun, you must understand its twelve *Kalas* (arts/phases). You see the moon; it completes itself in sixteen phases. For the Sun, there are twelve. 

Then there is *Om Um Somamandalaya Shodasha Kalatmane Namah*. Those of us who have practiced *Sadhana*, the advanced monks—they are complete in all sixteen phases. 

Then there is *Om Mum Vanhimandalaya Dashakalatmane Namah*. Fire has ten phases. So: twelve for the Sun, ten for Fire, and sixteen for great saints like us. That is the true *Omkar*. A-U-M. Not "Om," which is a derivative. 

It’s like the word *Chandra* (Moon), which became *Chandro*, then *Chand*. We use "Chand" now, but the root is *Chandra*. Similarly, *Aum* is the root. It rises from the navel and exits through the head. That vibration travels upward. There was a time when all work was done through the *Omkar* vibration. But now, that "blade" has become blunt. If you use a tool too much, it loses its edge. It needs to be sharpened, but we lack that now. 

But *Omkar* means "I am That." Trust me, and you will find peace. I am *Soham*. I am not saying *I* will give it to you; I am saying *you* will receive it if you believe. Those who have Self-knowledge can say, "Believe in me, have devotion, and you will receive everything." 

A person with such knowledge shouldn't really stay in this world. It is a world of sin. We say: *Paapo'ham Paapakarma'ham*—"I am sin, I perform sinful acts." If I use my Self-knowledge to help someone, Mother (the Divine) questions me. She asks, "Whom did you give it to?" We say, "Lord, you sent me to this world to help people. Let me not do anything so bad that I have to answer to you." Since I was born, I will have to answer for my life when I die. That is the most important thing—the accountability. So, Mother asks, "Whom did you help today?" 

As for being "certified" by others... there is a saying: "He who calls himself great is not truly great; he whom others call great is the one who is truly great." I cannot call myself God. But people will come from far and wide, from all over the world, and say, "You are God, please do this for me. You can do it; it is possible for you." You will hear this constantly.

**Anirban:** Gurudev, I want to ask one more thing. Regarding the sins we commit—is there such a thing as *Prayaschit* (atonement/penance)?

**Gurudev:** There is no such thing as atonement. What atonement can you do? If you murder someone, can you atone for it? Atonement is just a concept created by people to feel better. "Go perform this penance because you did this." But how can you erase what has already been done?

**Anirban:** So there is no way to atone for sins?

**Gurudev:** In my view, people just do it to satisfy their minds. After the Kurukshetra war, the Pandavas were troubled. They thought, "There is so much blood on our hands. We have killed so many people. How do we atone for this?" They asked their Guru. The Guru said, "Go to Benares (Varanasi). There are great scholars there; ask them if they can nullify your sins. I cannot."

So they went to Benares and met many saints. One suggestion they liked was the *Dashashwamedh Yagna*. Even today, you will see the Dashashwamedh Ghat in Benares where ten horse sacrifices were performed. After the ritual, they felt they were cleansed. They then set out for heaven. 

They had to pass through Kedarnath. When Lord Kedarnath (Shiva) saw them coming, he ran away in the form of a bull out of fear. Bhima managed to catch the bull by its tail. The place where the bull's hindquarters remained is Kedarnath, and the head appeared in Pashupatinath. The Lord said, "I will not touch you. You are terrible sinners; there is blood on your hands." 

So, did the atonement work? Answer me. I am giving you an instant answer. Atonement doesn't exist.

**Anirban:** But Gurudev, we hear that bathing during Makar Sankranti or the Kumbh Mela washes away sins...

**Gurudev:** No. Unless you have *Atmagyan* (Self-knowledge), which very few people have. If you commit a sin, you must face the consequences. Sin itself is like a god; it is the karma that demands to be felt. It will cause you pain, and then you will blame God, asking, "What did I do to deserve this punishment?" You saw that family today, their crying and their story... we see this every day. It breaks my heart. But there is no atonement for sin. Even the Pandavas couldn't do it, and their Guru was Krishna himself! If they couldn't, who are we? 

**Anirban:** I see. Thank you, Gurudev. And thank you to our viewers for being with us. We will end today’s episode here. Salutations, Gurudev.

**Gurudev:** Salutations.
