Gupt Sadhak Shama Khepa

Many years ago, before he attained the name Shama Khepa—even to him by his Guru—he was an exceptional businessman. He was very wealthy. We are talking about many decades ago; he is a hundred-plus now. Many decades ago, he had a firm—a law and audit firm—that he used to run, employing many lawyers and clerks. He had fifty employees under him. That was a big business. He had many cars and bungalows and lived an exceptional life.

One such client visit that he made to Nepal, he decided that early morning he would visit the Pashupatinath Temple. It should be mentioned that he was an intense Kali devotee, always in the sadhana of Ma Kali. Always mentally reciting her name. Even when he was walking, breathing, thinking, or at the time of business, her name would always run in his mind.

So, one such morning in Nepal, he walks into the temple. He manages to get into the garbhagriha in the early morning—you know how a businessman would be, exceptionally talented—and hugs the Shivalinga. He sits there, and that is when he feels an intense voice within him. It is not an external voice; it is within him. A voice telling him:

“You have been wasting your time. You are absolutely chasing after frivolous things. You were never meant for this. Leave this now and get out.”

He doesn’t waste a moment. Immediately, he hands over the entire business to his partner and walks out.

He was not a single man; he was married and had two children at that time. Imagine a man at the peak of his career—an exceptional businessman, so much money flowing around—suddenly decides as a married man, "I will now give off everything and become a simple monk."

From living in bungalows to immediately coming into a small hut. They could hardly afford sago (rice water). He had two children, and his wife never complained. Gurudev and his bhakti had become so intense that he would hardly be at home. But Guru Amma would make sure that the children were fed and watered in simplistic manners. And the most beautiful part is that they never actually ran out of money. When money would be required, it would somehow show up. When there were guests in the house, they would somehow find the money to buy fish and have all kinds of good food. So, Devi was always taking care of them, even though he had invoked the smashan (cremation ground) into his family’s life.

He would walk to Tarapith and he would be in sadhana all the time in Tarapith. Imagine, fifty or sixty years back, what kind of men would be doing the sadhana of Ma Tara in Tarapith. What intense sadhana these men would have carried in their blood.

But he was never satisfied with anybody there. He would walk and walk in the Maha Smashana. He would walk on the chitta (pyres). He would sit and meditate on the chittas, on burning dead bodies; surrounded by them, he would sit there. Years and years together, he would simply walk and become a wandering monk in the smashan.

One such day, there used to be a young boy who would only consume the mud from the smashan. It was said that he had cured his cancer merely by the consumption of the mud from the smashan. Gurudev noticed while passing him that he had suddenly fainted and was frothing at the mouth. Probably his end had come. But Gurudev was very kind. He immediately fetched a pail of water, bathed him, and started performing austerities and intense tapasya on him.

The boy recovered, opened his eyes, looked at Gurudev, and said, “Why have you done this? Your entire day's japa is wasted on reviving me. Why did you do this?”

To that, he said, “See, I had an opportunity to save your life. I have done it. What is there if I had lost one day's japa on you? One day's shakti on you? One day's sadhana on you? That bears no meaning on me. I am happy that your life is saved.”

The boy looks at him and says, “See, you will not find a Guru here. No matter how much you try, there is nobody here that is going to satisfy your thirst.”

He asks, “Why do you say that? There are so many intense sadhaks and tapasvis around me, talking to me, guiding me every moment. Why do you say that I will not find a Guru here?”

“No,” the boy says. “You must do the sadhana of Bama Khepa. For many years before you, he advanced in your sadhana.”

That is when he decides and gets into the sadhana of Bama Khepa.

Sometimes he would get up and start walking; an intense shakti would fill within him and he would simply start walking. While walking, he was never him. He would walk like the Smashan Bhairav.

“Just take one bigger asan today. Something is special about today. You take a bigger asan.”

Gurudev finds an older, bigger asan lying in the hut and he takes it. They both walk to the Ugra Tara Chitta. It is the middle of the night. They get into intense tapasya. Time passes. It is now deep in the night. Silence everywhere. Suddenly—intense thunder and lightning. Such an intense rain. And within minutes, he finds that where he is sitting on the Ugra Tara Chitta—it’s a mound, right? He is on top—everywhere up to his thigh, water has filled up.
