Introduction

Residual karma is nothing but the karma for which you are continuously taking birth; you are continuously in the cycle of life and death because of it. Someone who has burnt all their karma might still take birth, but only because there is a divine need for them to do so—there is a specific purpose. Like Avatars or great Rishis; they might still take birth despite being out of the Kalachakra (wheel of time) or Karma chakra, but they will eventually be back. There is a Dharmic reason why some souls continue to come back.

However, if you feel you are not part of that category—if you want to get out of the cycle of life and death—that is where the crux of Bhairava Sadhana begins.

The Historical Context

As Kali Yuga progresses, if you look at the last thousand years or so, it is majorly the Ugra Shaktis (fierce energies) that started taking a hit. During the times of the British, or anyone who felt these practices were extreme and wouldn't fit into societal norms, these deities were the first to be removed. In this category, the first few people they could list out were the ones that lived in the Smashanas (cremation grounds). The British obviously couldn't make sense of that. Why would somebody want to go and live in a Smashana? Because they found them impure, or they couldn't make conversation or sense out of it.

It was part of the Kalachakra of this land that all Ugra worships—all protection-oriented deities who had the energy and Shakti to defend this land from invasions—got decimated. They were meant to go. Over the last few centuries, you would find that Bhairava Upasana became smaller and smaller, while Soumya deities (gentle deities)—deities representing love, or whom even someone from an alien land could associate with—continued to flourish. Whereas Ugra deities started reducing.

However, somewhere around 2018—by my own personal calculations—the Kalachakra shifted. Post-2018, you would see a huge upswing in Dharmic activities and, subsequently, Bhairava Sadhana.

The God of Procedural Collapse

Bhairava is the God for Kali Yuga. He is a God that comes into his full form when there is a procedural collapse. When whatever was said to be the hard and fast rule—the Niyama—starts collapsing, that is where Bhairava flourishes.

Sorry to go back to that old story repeatedly, but that single story will tell you everything about Bhairava. He started—the deity sprouted out—by doing the biggest crime. As per Dharmic activities, killing a Brahmin or someone on the path of Dharma is considered the highest crime (Brahma-hatya). Even lowly humans wouldn't dare do something like that. But when Bhairava sprouted out, his first act was to take the head off none other than Brahma himself.

So, this is a deity who specifically resides when there is a collapse in the written processes and procedures—which is nothing but Kali Yuga. This is something you really need to get into your head to understand Bhairava. Whatever he makes you do, or the path he makes you take to reach him, you will find that everything seems to be what you thought you should not be doing. That is the crux of Bhairava.

The Nature of Offerings and Mindset

That is why the procedural offerings you see for Bhairava will never be what you generally offer other deities. Because wherever there is a procedural collapse, that is the space where Bhairava generally operates. He is at the final stage of realization wherein you realize that a cup of milk and a cup of something you generally would not take into a Puja room... there is no difference. When you reach that state of mind, that is when you begin to realize Bhairava. That is how his path is set.

The ultimate goal of Bhairava Upasana is to get out of the cycle of life and birth while living well. On one hand, you are continuously burning off all your residual karma carried from lifetimes past, and at the same time, you are not incurring any fresh karma that would require you to take birth again to burn.

This doesn't mean you need to live the life of a monk. No. You are alive in 2024; you live how you are meant to live in 2024. You will work on computers; you will get stuck in traffic jams. That is going to happen. It doesn't mean that to attain Bhairava you need to go sit in an ashram somewhere and not live life. It also doesn't mean that by trying to escape from all problems in life you are burning karma—you will just be back again. You need to get rid of the pent-up, residual karma incurred through your life.

Starting the Sadhana

We are all aware of the Ashtottara Namavali (108 names) of Bhairava. This is a good starting point. Go through some of the Stotras. Read the Ashtakams.

Kalachairava Ashtakam: You do not need any kind of initiation. In fact, you do not even need any major Upadesha (instruction) for that. Anybody can simply pick it up and make an entire Sadhana out of it. Daily Upasana of the Ashtakam is a very good starting point.

Then there are generic mantras like: Om Bhairavaya Namah. This specific mantra doesn't attach to any form; it is formless. Whichever form specifically attaches to your residual karma at this point in time, that mantra will take that form. If you are someone who has come to a state where you will be going through rapid progression, you will very soon come face-to-face with the Sadhana of Kala Bhairava because you are more or less done with your karma.

Everyone gets attracted to the highly graphical images of Kala Bhairava because he is depicted in his most fierce manner, and we get attracted to that aura. But it will not suit many of us. When we talk about karma, many of us have not evolved to a point where we can take the blame on ourselves.

As you progress in Bhairava Upasana, you will come to a point where you realize: "I am just a latent stone. Whatever happens around me is because I have earned it—be it good, be it bad."

It will come to a point where you realize whatever is happening to you—your father is not being good to you, your mother is unkind, or perhaps they are really good to you—all of this is incurred and earned karma. One way or the other, you have to burn it. If you have suffered anywhere in your life, be grateful for it, because you have done it. You have finished that part of your Kalachakra. You have gotten rid of it.

Graduating to Batuka Bhairava

The ultimate goal is to graduate through the forms of Bhairava and finally reach the Guru of Moksha, Kala Bhairava.

However, Om Bhairavaya Namah is good enough to keep going for a year. It doesn't attach to a form. But, the right form of Bhairava for you to start with is always going to be Batuka Bhairava.

Because he is a child. The form of a child is quick to forgive and also quick to punish. But the punishment will be like a child's: "I won't talk to you," or "I'll push you down." The energy is that of a child. You have to talk to him like you talk to a child. You have to show the same love. Generally, almost everyone I have met is able to handle Batuka Bhairava Upasana.

Prerequisites and Warnings

Ensure that you have completed another deity's Sadhana first. Have a little bit of history before touching Batuka Bhairava. Ideally, the Upasana of Sri Ganesha is a great starting point. Take a few Anushthanas of Ganesha, finish it successfully, take his blessings, and then move to Batuka Bhairava. Don't directly grab Bhairava and try to wrestle with him.

You need to understand that there are many beings without physical bodies who want Moksha, and they reside all over. The moment you start propitiating Bhairava, you will face this problem: you will think you are burning your karma and that is why you are having problems, but it is actually the Bhootas (spirits) surrounding you everywhere. It is the Pretas and Pisachas. You should be able to contain their energy too; they should not overwhelm you.

If they start overwhelming you, you will simply feel tired. You will feel depressed. That is not your karma. Feeling depressed is not you burning karma; something else is at play. Especially if you are in Bhairava Sadhana and feel depressed all the time and lack energy, you need protection.

To perfect a Batuka Bhairava Kavacham (armor/shield) is the fastest acting solution. Just start into it. Ensure you take up the Kavacham and recite it n number of times. At least once a day is compulsory. You can do it 10 or 20 times a day—whenever you are traveling or have time. Just read it.

Because any Bhairava Sadhana involves a lot of spiritual forces. Bhootas, Pretas, Pisachas. Many students need help with this because in Kali Yuga, people are strayed away from God and temples so much that they are "zoned out."
