Part 1: Kojagari Lakshmi Puja and its Significance

Disciple: My salutations, Gurudev.
Gurudev: Yes, blessings to you, my son.
Disciple: Gurudev, yesterday was Kojagari Lakshmi Puja. If you could tell us a bit about it...
Gurudev: Yes, Kojagari Lakshmi Puja fell on two days this time. The full moon (Purnima) began at 8 PM yesterday. Many performed the puja at 8, 9, or 10 PM. We call it "Kojagari," but the actual phrase is "Ke Jagari," which means "Who is awake?" The Mother says, "I will visit the home of whoever is awake." The rule is that the rituals are less important than staying awake through the night for the Mother. The name evolved from "Ke Jagari" to "Kojagari." Staying awake for the Mother all night is the most significant part. This tradition was very strong in the past, but it has almost disappeared now.
Disciple: Gurudev, how long does one stay awake? Is it until sunrise?
Gurudev: Exactly. Our day begins at sunrise. A day doesn't start before that. Usually, we sit for rituals one Danda (24 minutes) before sunrise. One should not sit before that. That is the essence of Kojagari—staying awake until dawn. This puja is particularly popular in East Bengal (now Bangladesh), but here, wherever Durga Puja is held, Lakshmi Puja is performed in the same marquee (pandal) after the immersion of Durga.
Disciple: Yes, it’s done in the same pandal.
Gurudev: Yes. While Kojagari Lakshmi Puja is often done as a community event, the household puja's main point is "Ke Jagari." Whoever stays awake for the Mother after the puja, She visits their home. That is the core message—staying awake all night with the lamps lit and the offerings intact.
Disciple: I’ve heard that the Mother loves to eat puffed rice, sweets (naaru), luchi, rice pudding, and halwa, and that she comes specifically to eat those?
Gurudev: Yes, that is right. Exactly.

Part 2: Errors in Durga Puja and Upcoming Kali Puja

Disciple: Salutations, Gurudev.
Gurudev: Yes, blessings, my son.
Disciple: Gurudev, Durga Puja has passed; it went reasonably well.
Gurudev: Don't say "well." What do you mean by "well"? Look at what happened. On the day of Navami, we had to "cut the thread" (adjust the rituals) in the almanac (Panjika) in the morning. After Ashtami and Sandhi Puja, there was so little time left that we were forced to finish things early. The "immersion" happened even before the actual immersion. We wait for the puja all year with joy, and usually, the days of Saptami, Ashtami, and Navami are spent in great happiness. That didn't happen this time. Even the priests couldn't perform the rituals properly. I have never seen such a strange alignment of lunar phases (Tithi) in my eighty years of life. I’ve never seen anything like it.
Disciple: Lakshmi Puja also ended around 4:20 PM today.
Gurudev: Well, Lakshmi Puja was spread over two days. Today, Thursday, was an auspicious day, so many did it today. The full moon started yesterday morning and lasted through the night and today. So there was no issue with Lakshmi Puja.
Disciple: Next is Kali Puja.
Gurudev: Yes, Kali Puja is coming. I hope it goes well for everyone. Kali Puja is a one-night affair and usually passes without issues. Previously, we held Kali Puja at the hospital. I don't know what will happen this year. However, Kali Puja isn't a major factor for us here because the worship of Mother Tara (Ma Tara) on the night of the New Moon (Amavasya) is our main focus. Kali Puja is done on a smaller scale.
