Part 2: Interview with Gurudev

(06:14) Interviewer: Gurudev, you mentioned there is a short story about Bama Deb Baba and Maa Annapurna. We also know that something similar happened regarding the Annapurna Puja at your own home. Could you tell us about that?

Gurudev: Yes. While I was practicing my spiritual discipline (Sadhana) at Tarapith, a gentleman named Lal Giri Baba arrived. He had a very striking appearance. He came to me and said, "Your navel has shifted; you must go to Kamakhya." At that time, I didn't quite understand what he meant by the navel shifting, though I do now. Anyway, he told me, "You must go to Kamakhya within a month." I told him I didn't have any money, so how could I go? He replied, "Don't worry about that, it will happen. Just go there and continue your practice."

True to his word, within a month, I found myself in Kamakhya. I stayed there for exactly three months. An elderly gentleman used to bring me food there; sometimes I ate, and sometimes I didn't. One day, he said to me, "Son, your work here is done. Now, perform a Kumari Puja here." So, on the day of Ashtami, he arranged for me to perform the Kumari Puja. After it was over, he called me aside that night and said, "Son, you must leave now. You need to go to Benares (Varanasi)."

I told him I had never been to Benares and didn't know the way. He said, "You don't need to know; you just go." So, I left Kamakhya and went to Benares. I chose Manikarnika Ghat to stay because I had a friend there who helped me settle in. It was right next to the cremation ground, near a temple of Lord Shiva. The Ganges water would often rise right up to where we were. He arranged for my stay and food.

I spent my days and nights in prayer. I visited the temples of the Mother and the Father (Shiva), recited the Chandi and the Gita. In those days, they used to give out two luchis and a bit of halwa as prasad. I survived on that alone. I had no money. I practiced my Sadhana while often being half-fed or fasting.

After nearly three months, I was sitting at Manikarnika Ghat, lost in thought, looking at the Ganges. An old woman with a walking stick approached me. She said, "Go, you don't need to stay here anymore. Go back home."

When I returned, I found that the Annapurna Ghat (the sacred pot) had already been placed at my home. A photo of the Mother had been set up, and the Puja had begun. I was stunned. Since then, we have continued this tradition for 30 years. Even today, the Mother comes and is worshipped, and many people come to eat.

However, this year is different. Because of the current situation [COVID-19 pandemic context], I couldn't invite anyone. People are afraid to come, and honestly, I want people to stay safe first. Religion comes after life. This disease is serious, and I don't want to go against science. I went to the police station and spoke to the Municipality Chairman. I told them I would perform the Puja very simply, without any pomp or crowds. No outsiders would be allowed. There are about 14 people in our household, and we are the only ones participating. I even put up a notice saying there would be no public Anjali or gathering.

But the Mother’s Puja must go on. As long as I am alive, I will try to continue it. It doesn't matter if people come or not; the Mother must be worshipped. It’s funny, every year, about a week before the Puja, an old woman I don’t know comes and gives me one or two rupees. That’s how I know the Mother is arriving.

Despite being a man of limited means, the Mother has never left me in debt. The Puja costs about 70,000 to 80,000 rupees these days, but somehow, she settles everything. We never ask her for anything; her presence is enough.

Part 3: Interview with Guruma (Gurudev's Wife)

(15:11) Interviewer: Guruma, pranam. We heard from Gurudev that this Puja has been running for 30 years. Since you joined this family, you’ve seen it every year. How is it organized?

Guruma: Pranam. Yes, we prepare many types of Bhog (food offerings). We offer food to Lord Shiva and the other deities. We have the Annapurna Bhog, Shiva Bhog, and the Pancha Devata (five deities) Bhog. In the month of Chaitra, we also perform Lakshmi Puja. We offer Bhog to Maa Kali in her room as well.

Interviewer: This year, due to special circumstances, the Puja isn't as grand. It must feel a bit sad.

Guruma: Yes, it feels a bit empty because there are no crowds or guests. But the rituals remain the same. We are preparing all the different types of Bhog and making all the arrangements. We have made sure there is no gathering, following the rules, but the heart of the Puja is still there.

Interviewer: Could you tell us what is included in the Bhog?

Guruma: We prepare many things. There is Sada Bhaat (white rice), Khichuri, Payesh (rice pudding), and fried rice. We make Sukto (a bitter vegetable stew) and nine types of fried vegetables. Then there are various curries: green jackfruit (Echor), cauliflower, cabbage, and potato-pumpkin curry. We also make dal (lentils) and raw mango chutney. We prepare a wide variety of dishes for the Mother.
