Host: We are listening to you on our YouTube channel, "Gupta Sadhak Shyama Khapa," about your Gurudev, Bama-deb Baba. You have been sharing various stories and your experiences from the life of Bama-deb Baba for the benefit of our devotees. We would like to hear more from you today.

Gurudev: Yes, certainly. There are many stories about Bama-deb Baba, but some are particularly remarkable—the kind that stay with you. For instance, consider Tara Khapa. He was Baba’s main disciple. Tara Khapa used to stay in various places and would occasionally come to see Gurudev. Whenever he came, Baba would become quite concerned because Tara Khapa was very fierce; he might hit or grab anyone. He always carried a staff and was truly formidable.

Once, Baba decided to cook a meal himself and feed some people. Arrangements were made, Baba finished the cooking, and everyone sat down to eat. Baba was serving the food with his own hands. Just then, Tara Khapa arrived. Seeing him approaching from a distance, Bama-deb Baba became quite anxious, thinking, "Tara is here; something is bound to happen now."

And indeed, something did. One of the people eating said to Baba, "Baba, could you give me a little more rice? And a bit of dal?" Baba had just lifted the ladle to serve him when Tara Khapa reached them. He shouted, "How dare you ask Gurudev for rice? Do you think he is your servant?" and he grabbed the man by the throat and slapped him.

Bama-deb Baba became very angry. He said, "Tara, if you don't leave this instant, something terrible will happen." Tara Khapa respected Bama-deb Baba immensely as his Gurudev, so he said, "Fine, I’m leaving." Then Baba said, "Wait, I need to send some money to someone; it would be good if you could deliver it." Baba used to send money to many schools back then—it was a time when schools struggled for funds. He would save up money and, whenever Tara Khapa visited, since he traveled to many places, Baba would tell him to deliver the funds so the schools could keep running. He also helped a widow who ran a school. Baba helped many people in many ways.

Once, there was a money chest (sinduk). Baba had made a hole in it so that any money offered would go straight inside. His disciples, like Nandu, Godai, and Nogen Kaka, would drop the coins in, and they would make a "tong-tong" sound as they fell. One day, it was noticed that money was missing from the chest. Baba didn't keep the key; he would leave it in a specific spot. There was a very poor man who couldn't afford to eat. Every morning, while Baba was bathing, this man would come, open the chest, take some money, and leave.

Word of this spread, and people started telling Baba, "Baba, we must do something about this. Your savings are disappearing." Despite their warnings, Baba didn't say anything. Eventually, a police case was filed. The local Daroga (inspector) was a devotee of Baba. Previously, there had been another case where Baba had been summoned. A police officer had come to investigate who took the money. Baba knew exactly who had taken it and why, even if no one else did, but he wouldn't say.

Baba was summoned to court to give testimony. They asked him, "Baba, is your money being stolen?" He replied, "Yes, it is being taken. But what is it to you? It’s my money; if someone is taking it, let them." They showed him the man and asked if he recognized him. Baba said, "I know him well. He is a very poor man who cannot afford to eat. He takes the money because of his hunger. Please, have mercy and release him. I don't mind; just let him go." Such was his compassion.

There is another story. Baba loved to smoke Ganja (cannabis) with his friends. One day, they couldn't find any. A boy said, "My father smokes; I’ll bring some." He brought a large quantity. They decided to smoke near a hay bale belonging to Sarkar Babu. As they smoked, a spark fell on the hay, and it caught fire. Everyone fled in fear. Bama-deb was right there, in the midst of the flames.

The fire raged, and everyone thought Bama-deb had perished. News reached his home, and his mother and father came running, along with many villagers. They were certain he was dead. However, about four or five hours later, Bama-deb was found playing near the Shimul tree. People were shocked. They said, "We saw you in the fire! You should be dead!" Two people confirmed they saw him in the flames.

When they asked him, he said, "You threw me into the fire, but my Tara Ma (Divine Mother) took me into her lap. It was so cool and peaceful there! She held me and told me to go play by the Shimul tree and that no one would hurt me." There are many such miraculous stories.

Gurudev: Yes, that happened many times. Baba was a master of "Kriya-yoga." Through Kriya, such things are possible. It is a true fact. Baba helped many people.

When he was young, he worked for a salary of two rupees under Daboke Kailashpati. But he didn't enjoy it and returned to Tarapeeth to "Boro Ma" (the Divine Mother). He used to call the deities "Choto Ma" and "Boro Ma." Daboke Kailashpati was his Gurudev, also a great saint. Once, a Shiva Linga was to be consecrated. Daboke was worried about who would perform the ceremony. Then it occurred to him, "Bama-charan is here; he is a living Shiva. Ask him." He sent people to fetch him, and Baba agreed.

At that time, no one in Daboke really knew Baba since he had left when he was very young. He arrived with Godai. There was a fair going on. A Sadhu called out to him, "Hey boy, come here!" Nandu and Godai told him not to go, but the Sadhu kept calling. When Baba went over, the Sadhu said, "I hear you like 'Pithe' (rice cakes)." He gave Baba a handful of raw rice and said, "Take this home and tell your mother to make Pithe for you." Baba took the rice, crushed it into fine powder in his palms, and gave it back, saying, "Now you eat it."

Later that night, during the consecration, a feast was held. The Sadhu was serving everyone. When he came to Baba, Baba said, "I won't eat yet; I have to consecrate the Shiva Linga." The Sadhu insisted, "Eat first, then do the ceremony." Baba finally sat down to eat. He started eating and just wouldn't stop. He ate everything served to him, and then he started taking food from the plates of those sitting next to him!

Word reached Daboke Kailashpati that someone was eating everything in sight. He suspected who it was. Baba was shouting, "Bring more! I'm still hungry! You invited me to eat, didn't you?" Daboke Kailashpati realized what was happening and sent a large bowl of cow's milk. He told them to give it to Baba. Baba drank the entire bowl in one go and finally said, "Ah, now my hunger is satisfied. My stomach is cool." There are so many stories like this.

I don't even want to speak too much about my Gurudev because he is my "Ishta" (deity), and it is often better to keep such things private. But what I have learned from books, from visiting his home, and from his descendants, I have shared. Most of it, however, I have experienced through my own association and spiritual practice. These are living truths; there is no doubt about them.

There is even a story about a man from Bangladesh who came to Baba. He was facing a very difficult legal case. He stayed with Baba for about a month, but nothing seemed to be happening. He became frustrated and angry. He shouted at Baba, "You help everyone else, but you've done nothing for me! I'm leaving!" Baba remained calm and simply said, "Go then. Go back home." The man demanded, "Give me something at least!" Baba said, "Just go."

When the man reached home, he found that the case had been settled in his favor and he had also received a large sum of money. He realized the greatness of Baba's silent work. He burst into tears, traveled all the way back from Bangladesh, and fell at Baba's feet. He said, "Baba, I didn't understand you." Baba simply said, "Who said I did anything? You did it yourself." There are countless such stories that show Baba’s glory. He was Lord Shiva himself.
