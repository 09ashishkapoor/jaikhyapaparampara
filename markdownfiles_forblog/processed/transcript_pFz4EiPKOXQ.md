Disciple: Greetings, Gurudev.

Gurudev: Greetings, my son.

Disciple: Tomorrow is the Snan Yatra (Bathing Festival). Could you please tell us something about the significance of this festival?

Gurudev: Snan Yatra is essentially the day for the ceremonial bathing of the deities. Among the four major pilgrimages (Dhamas), Puri Dham is considered the primary pilgrimage for the Kali Yuga. Lord Jagannath (Vasudeva), Lord Balabhadra (Sankarshana), Mother Subhadra, and Mother Bimala reside there.

As you may know, it is said that Lord Krishna's heart is placed within the chest of Lord Jagannath. It still beats with a rhythm, just like a human heart.

Disciple: How can one understand this?

Gurudev: Those who perform the daily worship and are close to the deity can feel it. Even Lord Jagannath’s blood pressure is said to fluctuate. Every day, a large bowl of sandalwood paste (Chandan) is applied to His body.

Disciple: Is there a specific reason for that?

Gurudev: There is no complex reason; He simply loves sandalwood. It is cooling and soothing. The paste is applied over His entire body and then removed in the evening once it dries. This sandalwood is often distributed or sold to devotees as it is considered very sacred and beneficial. I have tasted it myself—it is wonderful and leaves a sweet fragrance in the mouth.

Disciple: You mentioned tasting it. Is there a specific benefit to consuming it?

Gurudev: It is believed to remove impurities from the body. Lord Jagannath's sandalwood is incredibly pure and fragrant. It eliminates any bad odor and is spiritually uplifting.

Now, regarding the Snan Yatra tomorrow: Lord Jagannath will have His ceremonial bath, and the temple will remain closed to the public. An amazing thing happens during this time—when Lord Jagannath is bathed, every deity in every temple across India, including the 51 Shakti Peethas, is bathed at that exact same moment.

If you go to Kalighat tomorrow, you’ll notice they don’t allow the public to watch. A curtain is drawn. During this time, the "Mami" (the sacred relic or body part of the Goddess) is taken out and bathed with many pots of Ganga water. Similarly, Lord Jagannath is bathed with numerous pots of water.

Because so much water is poured over Him, Lord Jagannath actually falls ill. He develops a fever. This is something only those who serve Him closely can truly know. His heart continues to beat, and His physical state changes. When He falls ill, a special herbal decoction called Pachan is prepared in large quantities and offered to Him to cure the fever. It takes seven days for Him to recover.

Disciple: So, the fever is caused by the excessive bathing?

Gurudev: Yes. This period is known as Ashuddha Kaal (the period of impurity or ailment). The priests (Pandas) maintain this tradition strictly. For seven days, He suffers from the fever until the Pachan takes effect.

While there is a spiritual side to it, think about it logically—if that much water were poured over a human's head, they would surely fall ill. The same happens to Lord Jagannath. During the bath, the doors are closed. Across the 51 Peethas, the relics are brought out, bathed, and reinstalled while the priests are often blindfolded.

At Kalighat, you will see seven priests performing the ritual behind a curtain, and their eyes are blindfolded. They take the sacred relic, bathe it with Ganga water, and put it back. This happens simultaneously across India at the time of the Jagannath bath.

Disciple: Gurudev, there is a rumor that Lord Jagannath will not board His chariot (Ratha) this year. Is there any connection?

Gurudev: According to the Bhavishya Malika (a book of prophecies), starting from 2023, the Lord will not board the chariot for six years. This is because a period of great destruction (Mahapralay) has begun. It actually started in 2019, but it will intensify now. The number of deaths from accidents and disasters will double. The Mahapralay will truly escalate once Lord Jagannath leaves the temple. It is said that eventually, the ocean will swallow the temple. Until then, Lord Hanuman is said to be protecting it.

Disciple: Gurudev, what about the pillar (Sthambha) at the Lion's Gate (Singhadwara)? Is that a deity?

Gurudev: No, that pillar indicates the level. It shows that the Lord is seated at that specific elevation from the ground.

Regarding His food—when Lord Jagannath stayed with the Sabara (tribal) people in the forest, He ate what they ate: coarse rice and a vegetable mash (Chorchori) made of wild roots and tubers. To this day, His main offering consists of coarse rice and vegetables cooked without salt, because the tribal people did not use salt.

While Lord Balabhadra (who is considered an incarnation of Shiva) is offered 56 types of delicacies (Chhappan Bhog), Lord Jagannath’s own preference remains that simple tribal food. He is a deity without vanity.

Disciple: So, even during Snan Yatra, Subhadra and Balabhadra are bathed as well?

Gurudev: Yes, all three are bathed. Although we call it "Jagannath’s Bath," all three participate, just as all three have their own chariots during the Chariot Festival.

Disciple: Can ordinary people perform this ritual at home?

Gurudev: No, this is not an ordinary ritual. It is extraordinary and highly restricted. No one is allowed to see the actual bathing of the main relics. Even the priests are blindfolded. It is said that seeing the exposed "heart" or the main relic could be fatal for a person because the energy is so intense. That is why the doors are closed and the priests cover their eyes.

Disciple: We have learned so much today, Gurudev. Many things were unknown to us. Thank you.

Gurudev: Blessings to you, my son. Blessings.
