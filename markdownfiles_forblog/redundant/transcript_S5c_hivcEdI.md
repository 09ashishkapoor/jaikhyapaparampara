[Atmospheric sounds of a conch shell and bells ringing in the temple]

Narrator: It is the night of the Kali Puja New Moon (Amavasya). You are watching the Mother in the temple. A Hom (ritual fire) is currently being performed; let me show you.

[Sounds of Sanskrit chanting continue]

Narrator: Here is Bamdev Baba. He was established here on the day of our annual puja. Now, I am heading to see my Guru.

Narrator: Guru, my salutations (Pranam) to you.

Guru: Pranam, my son. It is quite an atmosphere for Kali Puja. One Hom was completed early this morning, and another is underway now. This happens every year.

Narrator: I want to ask you something. Kali Puja and the New Moon are deeply connected to Tantra. Do those who practice Tantra do good or bad work on this night?

Guru: They do all kinds of work. I actually forbid everyone to even look at the Mother on the night of the New Moon. I warn people because no good comes from it; it is a night when a great deal of harmful work is done.

As humans, we are defined by three qualities: Sattva (goodness/purity), Rajas (passion/action), and Tamas (darkness/ignorance). On the night of Kali Puja, the Satvik (pure) way is rarely followed. Many rituals involve things like fish, meat, and the heads of animals like jackals. These are not Satvik practices; they are Tamasik.

The Rajasik (regal/passionate) path is also followed here, but the Satvik path is not common for Kali Puja. Those who follow the Satvik path do so differently; they are great souls who do not involve themselves with blood, fish, or meat.

The Tamasik path involves the Panchamakara (the five "M"s): wine, meat, fish, parched grain, and sexual intercourse. Great sages may internalize these practices within their own bodies to reach higher states, but there is another group—those I call Pisach (demonic/spirits). They bring in external items—meat, wine, and blood—to attract people. These things are not good; those who practice this are what I consider Pisach.

Narrator: In that context, there is also something called Pisach Tantra, right?

Guru: Yes, absolutely. There are practices like Dakini Vidya, Yogini Vidya, and others. They are as dangerous as they sound. They use these to harm people.

Narrator: Are these practices like Dakini or Pisach Tantra always used to harm people?

Guru: Yes, they are used for bad purposes. They use these powers against people to do harm. That is why I say: do not look at the Mother on the New Moon. It is a dangerous time. Our elders used to tell us not even to move from our houses on this night.

Those who are Satvik perform their puja through meditation and internal reflection; they don't need all these external things. But those who are Tamasik use these powers to harm others. That is their only goal.

Narrator: There is also Mohini Tantra.

Guru: Yes, Mohini, Yogini—there are many. And the most common one lately is black magic (Kala Jadu). This is also used for harm. I see many people coming to me because someone has tried to harm them using these methods. They take a person’s hair or a piece of their clothing to perform these rituals. It’s very popular right now, but it will fade eventually. It is never used for good.

Narrator: So, on this night of Kali Puja, why is there such a massive crowd at Tarapith and in the cremation grounds?

Guru: It shouldn’t be that way. I repeat: householders (Grihis), both men and women, should not go there on this night. It is not a place for them today. People don't understand, and then they end up in trouble and come to people like me. I scold them, but they still come, calling me "God" and asking for help.

Narrator: Guru, when you help these people who have been affected by black magic or other Tantric harms, do you have to take that "bad energy" onto yourself? How does it go away?

Guru: I do have to take it upon myself. Because we work in the name of God, these dark forces fear us. I have seen cases where simply touching a trident or placing a hand on someone makes the dark energy leave them. But it takes a toll. I have been hospitalized five times because of this.

People use women for rituals, they use wine... they think Kali Puja cannot happen without wine and that Shiva needs weed. That is all nonsense. They drink it themselves and then blame it on the gods to shift the responsibility. The gods are Satvik beings; they don't need those things.

I help people for free. In other places, people spend hundreds of thousands of rupees. I tell them, "You have money, so you gave it, but I don't need your money."

I warn everyone again: householders should stay inside. On the night of the New Moon, stay home. Don't be lured out by anyone. This is even mentioned in the Chandi: despite knowing the truth, people still fall into the pit of delusion (Mohagarta).

Narrator: Guru, to clarify for the audience, you don't see people on Saturdays, Sundays, New Moons, Full Moons, or Ekadashi. Is that correct?

Guru: Yes, that is correct. And starting from the 4th of November, I will be at the Rajpur cremation ground from 4:00 PM to 5:00 PM, but I will not see more than ten people. I am not well, and neither is the Guru-Ma. If I try to clear too much negative energy right now, it affects me too deeply.

Narrator: You have heard the Guru’s words. There is a lot to learn here. We grew up hearing these things, but now we are seeing the reality.

Guru: People are digging their own graves. I pray that everyone stays safe.

Narrator: Happy Diwali to everyone. Stay healthy and stay well. Guru, my salutations.

Guru: Blessings to you.
