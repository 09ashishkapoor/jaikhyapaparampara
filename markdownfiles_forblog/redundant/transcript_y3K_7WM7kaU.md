Interviewer: Gurudeb, my respects to you.

Gurudeb: Blessings, son. Speak.

Interviewer: It has been a long time. Today is Amavasya (the new moon). After many days, Shanti Kunj is quiet—there isn't much of a crowd around you.

Gurudeb: About 10 or 15 people are sitting there.

Interviewer: I’d call that quiet because they aren't here for an audience today; they just came. It’s hard to find a day like this. Anyway, Gurudeb, I wanted to discuss something else today. Many young men and women—as well as older people—come to you saying, "Gurudeb, I want to take Diksha (initiation) from you."

Why is the younger generation so attracted to you right now? First, I want to know about that. And also, what exactly is Diksha? Since childhood, we’ve heard the words "Shiksha" (education) and "Diksha" used together.

Gurudeb: Exactly. Where there is Shiksha, there is Diksha. One who has true education can have initiation. Without education, there is no initiation. They are linked. People often say in common speech, "You have no education or initiation," or "Your parents didn't give you any education or initiation." So, the two are deeply connected.

Now, what does the word Diksha mean? It means purifying the body—purifying the body and mind—and fostering personal development. That is a significant thing. Initiation brings development. Many people fall into trouble and think that taking initiation will solve their problems. That is actually true. If you have the right Shiksha, Diksha will follow.

In this process, there are two people: one who gives and one who receives. For the one who receives, it’s simple; they take it and then go around telling everyone, "I took initiation from Shyama Khepa!" It becomes a matter of pride for them. Hearing this, more people come. They hear someone say, "I got initiation yesterday from Shyama Khepa; it didn’t cost a penny." This spreads, and the crowds grow.

Even the younger generation is slowly turning toward God. Previously, young people only said, "Eat, drink, and live!" They’d ask, "What’s the point of calling on God? Does God even exist?" This happened in the past, too, even during the time of Trailanga Swami. There were wicked people who didn’t believe in God.

Let me tell you a story. Trailanga Swami was sitting in his cave one day. A group of "bad" people came and asked, "Father, we’ve come to you to find out if God exists. Is there a God?" Swami told them, "Wait, let me see." Then, inside the cave, he asked, "God, are you there?" A voice replied, "No, I am not."

Swami told them, "See? You were right. God doesn't exist." They were very satisfied and left. Then a group of "good" people came and asked the same thing: "Father, we want to know if God exists." Swami asked again, "God, are you there?" The voice replied, "Yes, I am here."

Swami told them, "See? He is here." So, both "Yes" and "No" came from the same source. Swami initiated many people. If you go to his room, you’ll see many words and teachings written on the walls with chalk. They are very important teachings.

Interviewer: We had those teachings here too, on the walls. I had them printed out and laminated because of the crowds. Many people would see them and take photos. We eventually had to take them down because of the construction, but they were very good.

Gurudeb: Now, the word "Guru" implies a heavy "Guru-responsibility" (a play on words, as Guru also means heavy/significant in Bengali).

Interviewer: Yes, the saying goes, "I have taken on the Guru-responsibility."

Gurudeb: A Guru has a duty. He must fulfill it. If he doesn't, he cannot be called a Guru. What is that duty? First, he must consider the condition of the person taking initiation. He should not take a single penny from them. I tell people beforehand: "Don't give money." Some try to give, but we don't accept. There are many poor people who tell me they weren't given initiation elsewhere because they didn't have money. I tell them, "Come here, bring a small offering (like a piece of fruit), and I will initiate you."

I have written it clearly here at the temple: "Do not give a single penny." I wrote that with pride. Yet, there are some people who still ask for money from visitors. But does the Mother (Goddess) need to beg? Does she need you to beg to feed her? She is feeding you! And yet people proudly say, "I am feeding the Mother rice and lentils." There is no need for that. God provides His own food, and He even arranges food for others.

By the Mother's grace, someone always shows up to provide.

Interviewer: I have seen this with my own eyes. One day we were worrying about how the ritual would happen because there was no rice or lentils. We were sitting there in the afternoon, and suddenly a gentleman arrived in a car. He dropped off a sack of rice and a sack of lentils, bowed to the Mother, and left without saying a word.

Gurudeb: See? That’s what I mean. Sacks of rice and lentils just appeared. It's as if the Mother said, "What are you worrying about?"

Anyway, a Guru has a responsibility. We look out for those who cannot eat. We hear from many people who call us about financial struggles or illnesses. The only problem is that it is very difficult to leave the body. To project the soul out of the body to go and check on people is hard. The body stays behind. Then you have to finish the work and re-enter the body. Both leaving and entering are very critical.

Interviewer: Gurudeb, since you mentioned this, could you shed some light on it? You said you leave your body and then return, and you check on your disciples to see if they are okay because you took on that responsibility.

Gurudeb: Yes, I am saying it is possible. It’s not an impossible thing at all. Many people have seen me at their homes.

Interviewer: I have heard this from many people myself!

Gurudeb: One gentleman invited me to his new house. I thought, "It’s so far away, how will I go?" But that night, I went. I even ate at his house! He served me Posto (poppy seeds). Later, he sent a large amount of Posto here, saying, "Gurudeb, I know you love Posto." I asked him how he knew, and he said, "Because you came to my house and ate it!" That was in America! He sent it from there because he knew I liked it.

Interviewer: That’s just one story; there are so many! Every day, people say, "You came to me," or "I saw you."

Gurudeb, when is this possible for a human being? I see you as a human, but in my heart, I feel I am sitting before God.

Gurudeb: It’s not possible for everyone. Only true seekers know, and they never disclose it. There are thousands of proofs. People tell me, "You came and took my cycle." I’ve heard someone in Siliguri say, "You came on a bicycle!" I try to brush these things aside. I don't want these things to be discussed too much in public. I prefer to move them to the side.

As I said, for the disciple, it’s easy, but the Guru carries the burden.

Interviewer: But doesn't the disciple have a duty? If I am initiated by you, don't I have a responsibility?

Gurudeb: No, your duty is to look after your parents, your children, and your wife. Why should your money go to the Guru?

Interviewer: I don't mean feeding the Guru. I mean, after taking initiation, what should I do?

Gurudeb: You should chant (Japa). Call on God. Constantly call on the Guru and God. That is your work. Your work is not to worry about what the Guru is eating. People who do that get stuck; they can't progress. A disciple who doesn't keep a spiritual connection is a "real" disciple.

Out of a thousand initiations, maybe two or three true disciples emerge who actually focus on chanting and spiritual practice. They call me to ask if their chanting is correct.

If I initiate someone, the responsibility for their well-being falls on me. If they are in danger, I have to look out for them. Also, we don't like to eat at a disciple's house. If we accept their food and don't do spiritual work for them, we get bound. If a Guru cannot do that, he isn't a Guru. A Guru should never be greedy.

A greedy Guru will face trouble. Let me tell you a short story. A Guru had a disciple who was very poor. Because he had no money, the Guru kept him at a distance. The disciple would come every day, but the Guru ignored him. The disciple thought, "Since I have no money, I should go farm." He went to his land, worked hard, and became very wealthy.

One day, the Guru heard about this and decided to visit. He went to the disciple's house and liked everything he saw. The disciple’s wife didn't know the history, so she welcomed him. The Guru started pointing at things—a cloth, some jewelry—saying, "The Mother would like this." The disciple gave them all. Then the Guru saw a granary full of rice and asked for that too.

The disciple’s wife was getting angry. When it was time for the Guru to leave, the disciple came to him and said, "Gurudeb, I want to cut off your two feet and keep them here." The Guru was shocked! The disciple said, "The Guru’s feet are my refuge (Guru-pada bharosa). If I have your feet, I have everything. So I will cut them off and keep them." The Guru got so scared he ran away, leaving everything behind!

That is a greedy Guru. Such people shouldn't be Gurus. You must look carefully before choosing a Guru.

Interviewer: That brings me to my next point. Just as a Guru examines a disciple, a disciple should examine the Guru.

Gurudeb: If a Guru isn't greedy, what is there to examine? He will give. People come to me saying, "I haven't received initiation anywhere else, I worship Kali, please help me." I give it to them, even if I know they might not do much with it.

Interviewer: Is there such a thing as a connection from a past life (Janmantar)? If I have you as my Guru in this life, will I have you in the next?

Gurudeb: Yes, it starts again from where it ended. You will find me again. It takes seven or eight lifetimes to reach completion. A Guru knows who will progress and who won't. I have some disciples who are practicing in Pashupatinath, some in Kamakhya. An Aghori Tantric from Mayong came here once; he said Mahadev (Shiva) told him to come.

It is a very difficult path. The Guru-disciple relationship is rare. The Guru helps from behind. I have about 8 or 10 disciples who are deeply into their practice in places like Benares and Kamakhya. People know me all over the world. I am everyone’s Gurudeb—even for those I haven't officially initiated. I get calls from Canada!

But as I said, it’s a difficult path. These days, some Gurus just follow their disciples around trying to take their money. That’s not right.

Interviewer: Gurudeb, we’ve heard so many wonderful things today. Many people are waiting outside. They’ve realized that there is no way forward without God or a Guru. There are 50 people in line.

Gurudeb: Because of the heat, I can't give initiation right now. Tell them to leave their names, and we will call them when the weather is better. My health isn't great either. Giving initiation takes a lot of energy and responsibility. I’m getting older.

Interviewer: I won't make this any longer. We’ve heard enough for today. I hope everyone stays well and healthy. My respects, Gurudeb.

Gurudeb: Blessings, son. Blessings.
