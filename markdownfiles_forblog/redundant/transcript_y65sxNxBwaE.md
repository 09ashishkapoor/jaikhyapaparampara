Female Voice-over: Salutations to the venerable hidden ascetic, Sri Sri Syama Khapa. Greetings! We begin our program from Shantikunj with a bow to our Guru, the hidden ascetic Sri Sri Syama Khapa. Let us all listen to the invaluable words of the Guru together.

[00:32 - 00:33] Interview

Interviewer: Guru Dev, Pranam (salutations).

Guru Syama Khapa: Pranam, my son.

Interviewer: Many of our friends on the Guptasadhak Syama Khapa YouTube channel are curious about various topics. One subject many have expressed interest in is Balak Brahmachari. Could you please shed some light on Balak Brahmachari in this episode?

Guru Syama Khapa: Look, he was a realized soul since birth. He is known as "Janmasiddha" (perfected from birth) Sri Sri Balak Brahmachari. His father’s name was Surendra Chandra Chakraborty. He was born into a highly respected family, and everyone in that lineage was a staunch believer in God. His mother was Smt. Charushila Devi. His grandfather was Binabandhu Thakur, and his uncle was Bhagabati Thakur. On his mother’s side as well, everyone was a pillar in the field of religion; no one was less than the other.

He was a realized soul from birth. Stories of his entering Samadhi (spiritual trance) were heard many times even when he was a small child. When he played with his friends, he would suddenly go into Samadhi. His friends would pour water on him to bring him back. He went into Samadhi countless times. Just as Ramakrishna Paramahansa would enter Samadhi during casual conversations, he was very much of that type.

To explain further, I have to touch upon many things, but I will keep it brief. He was the greatest follower of Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose. He met Netaji many times and had long discussions with him. Inspired by Netaji, he later took the Vedas as his guide. He wanted one thing above all: the India that Netaji envisioned. He wanted to liberate India from this rotten, decaying society and the constant infighting and chaos. Netaji once said to keep India under military rule for ten years; Balak Brahmachari shared that same belief.

Using the Vedas as his foundation, he wanted to create something on Indian soil. He said something very profound: "Whether you are a king or a commoner, if you want to enjoy the spoils, everyone must pay the price." This was the core of his philosophy. If you want to indulge, you must suffer the consequences.

If you look at the history of Balak Brahmachari, his father was Surendra Chakraborty, his mother was Charushila Devi, and he was born on November 9, 1920, in Medhimandal village, Bikrampur, Bangladesh.

I know for a fact that he was very close to Netaji. They spoke many times. He wanted to organize India exactly how Netaji wanted it. It is unfortunate that when he left his body in Sukhchar, his body was brought out much later. At that time, his devoted follower Subhas Chakraborty, who regarded him as his Guru, was the one who eventually moved the body.

Personally, I feel he went into a deep Samadhi and left for another realm. I don't believe he "died" in the conventional sense. I think he went to "Gyan Gunj," the same place where Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose is said to still exist. He wanted a "pocketless," saffron-clad monk to rule India based on the Vedas.

He relied on the Vedas for everything. He wrote the teachings of the Vedas on walls and published many books. I can name a few of his followers, like Shankar Sarkar from Rajpur. He presented me with a book titled Kora Chabuk (The Hard Whip). It is a book of questions and answers where he provided explanations based entirely on the Vedas. He never strayed from the Vedic path. He tried his best to elevate the Sanatana Dharma because the strength of the Hindus lies in the Vedas.

He wanted to build a society in India that was free from corruption—a liberated India.

Interviewer: You mentioned the Vedas and Upanishads, and you mentioned Netaji and Balak Brahmachari. This involves "Karma Yoga." The Indian society of that time was quite advanced.

Guru Syama Khapa: Absolutely. It was a society based on equality. He had studied history extensively. He lived through the Congress era and the CPM era. I saw many CPM youths who were his devotees. One of them, Shyamal Debnath, was a great devotee. He used to come to me and share many stories about Guru Dev.

The government couldn't accept many things, just as they couldn't accept Netaji. They couldn't accept him either. But he truly wanted a beautiful, free society. He had entered Samadhi once before in Jadavpur, which caused a stir, and then he moved to Sukhchar. He traveled all over India and the world and had many disciples.

I feel sad sometimes because he had so many followers. I don't think anyone in modern society had as many disciples. Lokenath Baba, Bhamdeb Baba, and Ramakrishna had many, but he gained so many so quickly. There was something about him that attracted people instantly. Perhaps it was the cycle of time, or perhaps society doesn't have room for good people. When good people interfere with the interests of certain groups, they are not spared.

He traveled the world. Even today, his birthday is celebrated with music. Shankar Sarkar, who was very close to him, still keeps a room dedicated to Guru Dev in his house. No one else is allowed to enter. Guru Dev's bed and bathroom are kept exactly as they were. Shankar tells me that Baba still wants to eat at his house every day, and whatever he feels Baba wants, he cooks and offers it.

Shyamal Debnath is also a great devotee. He once asked me, "Why do you perform Kali Puja?" I didn't answer him at the time; I just listened. He is a staunch devotee, so it's better not to argue. He told me about the separate arrangements for Baba and how he still "eats" there. It’s heartening to know that in our village of Rajpur, there is such a devoted soul.

Many things are being lost. People don't study these figures anymore; they have become "anti-establishment." But if anyone reads his teachings, they will be inspired. There is politics involved, of course—he hated modern politics. I've read parts of his book, and although I couldn't finish it because it's so vast, I understood his essence.

Interviewer: Have you ever met him in person?

Guru Syama Khapa: Yes, I have seen him face-to-face. He used to come here in a car. He once came behind this cremation ground. A huge crowd gathered. He gave initiation to many people. I personally didn't quite like the way he gave initiation, but I saw so many young men following him. Wherever I mentioned his name, people held him in high regard. Just like Netaji was "banned," he was also marginalized. But his followers are everywhere, and many are quite wealthy.

I’ll tell you a story. Shyamal Debnath once caught a lot of fish in his pond. At 3:00 AM, he took those fish to Sukhchar. But he wasn't allowed in. From the ground floor to the third floor, there were armed guards. When they told the guards they brought fish from their own pond for Guru Dev, the guards just told them to leave the fish there and go. They weren't allowed to meet him. There were many young men on duty to protect him.

We all know what happened at Sukhchar; it wasn't that long ago—maybe 15 or 20 years.

Interviewer: About the book Kora Chabuk—in today’s society, moral decay is very evident.

Guru Syama Khapa: That really bothered him. The decaying state of society pained him deeply. He dreamt of a new India based on the Vedas and Upanishads, just like Netaji did. He felt that those who are contributing to this decay need to be "whipped," and that's the "Hard Whip" of moral correction.

In those days, you would see "Ram Narayan Ram" written on every wall. He coined that name by breaking down the "Omkara." Everything was Vedic. But today, in this Kali Yuga, as Vyasa Dev predicted, Vedic studies are disappearing. People no longer read the Vedas, Upanishads, or the Gita.

The last great saint of Tarapith was Shankar Khepa. You must have heard of him. He also used to bow at Balak Brahmachari’s feet. This isn't just hearsay; it’s a fact. Shankar Khepa had immense respect for him. He was a great devotee of Mother Tara, yet he bowed to Balak Brahmachari.

Many people were close to him. If you listen to his Vedic discourses, you will be energized. But he disappeared suddenly. Maybe he knew it was time. There are many "dummies" or decoys. Whether the one who was cremated was a decoy or not, I cannot say. Just as King Jong-il was said to have decoys, these great saints often do. Netaji had decoys, as seen in the Gumnami Baba case. I personally believe he is still alive in Gyan Gunj.

I haven't finished the book because it's so thick and filled only with questions and answers. But if people read it—well, people don't feel the need to read anymore. Society has reached such a state of decay that only the Vedas can save it. But where are the people to lead that? What we are saying is just based on what we've read. People talk about "Hindu awakening," but they don't even understand what "Hindu" means. They haven't studied the Indus Valley Civilization or history.

He wanted "Hindutva," but not in a narrow sense. The strength of Hindus is the Vedas, Muslims have the Quran, and Christians have the Bible. He wanted to move forward with the Vedas.

Interviewer: You have said in various places that a great man will arise from this Bengal to rejuvenate human civilization.

Guru Syama Khapa: Yes, he will. He will definitely arise from this Bengal.

Interviewer: You spoke about Balak Brahmachari today. Are you hinting at anything?

Guru Syama Khapa: I am definitely hinting at something. We have lost the Vedas. We don't read the Upanishads or the Gita. Believe me, where the Gita is recited, there are no people. No one asks, "What did you read?" This is a great advantage for priests today. If you ask them a question, they say, "What will you understand? You are a householder." I've seen priests who can't even explain the meaning of the Gita themselves.

In that regard, I've seen Shankar Sarkar. His Gita is memorized. He has traveled to various countries to recite the Gita. He is very well-versed in the Vedas and Gita.

Balak Brahmachari’s disciples come to me because I speak frankly. He gave so many initiations; if those disciples truly awakened, there would be no problem. But for some reason, he was silenced. People are trying to silence me as well, in various ways, so I don't speak about the Vedas. If I recite the Vedas, I get 10 viewers. If I talk about "Tantrik" stuff, I get 10,000 views in a second. People’s character has changed. They want to hear about ghosts, skulls, and external appearances.

He had no specific dress code. Look at this picture in the book—anyone would be inspired. He looks like a deity. He is a part of the Divine, a realized soul. Look at how he was respected and loved wherever he went. It’s a mystery why such a man left. I believe he is still here, perhaps not as Balak Brahmachari, but in another form. He knew everything that was going to happen. He was always thinking about Netaji. Netaji is in Gyan Gunj, and he is likely there too. They will return when the Divine commands it.
